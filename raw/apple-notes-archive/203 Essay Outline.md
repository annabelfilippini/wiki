Each Olympics has a host city, more formally known as the Organizing Committee of the Olympic Games (OCOG). These cities are go through an extensive and expensive bidding process and are then required to meet extremely high expectations of athletes, coaches, spectators, the IOC, etc.  Because of the expensive and the Olympic requirements fewer and fewer cities have been applying to host the Olympics. This is leading for the need to change the overall bidding process. 

- What does the bidding process look like 
	- The bidding preparation begins 12-14 years in advance and the election for the host city takes place 6-7 years prior to the game. 
	- Prior to the bidding process cities must develop an image and a theme. They must clearly outline what their legacy is going to be after the games so the IOC knows the impact the games will have on this particular city. 
		- Each candidate city delivers a 1 hour long presentation to the ICO membership and the elimination process beings 
			- Each IOC member votes for their favorite city and the city with the least number of votes is eliminated and this continues until 2 cities remain. Then there is a final vote with the 2 cities to make the overall decision. 
	- The cost of planning, hiring consultants, organizing events and the necessary travels usually falls between $50 and $100 million. 
		- Cities usually spend 2-3 times more than expected on the bid. Tokyo spent as much $150 million on its failed 2016 bid. 

- After all of this why would a city still want to host?
	- The Olympics are usually a catalyst for urban regeneration. They boost the economy. Cities usually feel good about themselves for hosting the games because they are essentially bringing the world together. This is a great opportunity to showcase cities and to spread their cities culture.
