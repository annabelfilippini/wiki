# BB Session: Wayloft Landing Page Design Direction

**Date:** 2026-04-04
**Agent:** bb-design
**Project:** wayloft
**Skill:** /design-consultation

## Context

Landing page redesign. Current page leads with Worth-It tool. Annabel wanted full platform introduction instead — premium, aspirational, private-lounge/steel-credit-card vibe. Four reference screenshots reviewed: Stitch (premium light), Jesko Jets (aspirational aviation), steel card render, dark agency.

## Key Decisions

- **Page purpose:** Full platform introduction, not Worth-It conversion funnel. Landing page sells the dream; app delivers the instrument. Worth-It becomes a nav item, not the hero.
- **Hero style:** Abstract premium hero — clean light background with steel credit card photo + large type. Stitch energy. Premium without requiring hero photography that could feel stock. Cards ARE the visual.
- **Headline:** "Your points, properly spent." Confident, understated, premium positioning. From the Stitch mockup Annabel built herself — already validated.
- **Typography:** Playfair Display for landing page headings + body, creating cohesive editorial/old-money feel. Data/labels stay Geist Mono, buttons stay Geist sans. Landing page intentionally diverges from Signal app aesthetic. *(Note: this was later reverted in code — font direction unresolved.)*

## Killed / Deferred

- **Worth-It-as-hero (Approach A)** — killed. Landing page job is to introduce the platform, not funnel to one tool.
- **Photography hero** — rejected. Stock photo risk. The steel card render is more dramatic and authentic.
- **AI-generated card art in hero** — killed. CardArtPlaceholder gradient cards look AI-generated. Real image needed.

## Open Questions

- Landing page typography: Playfair was tried and reverted. What's the right serif (or non-serif) approach?
- How does the landing page aesthetic bridge to the Signal design system in-app?
