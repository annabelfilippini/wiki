# The Year Ahead 2026: Optimism with Footnotes

By Bill Gates, published January 9, 2026, Gates Notes.

## Overview

Gates frames 2026 as a pivotal year where the innovation pipeline and choices made now will set up the decades to come. He remains optimistic because of AI-driven innovation, but his optimism "comes with footnotes." He poses three key questions that will determine the trajectory of progress.

## Key Claim: Child Deaths Went Up

"The thing I am most upset about is the fact that the world went backwards last year on a key metric of progress: the number of deaths of children under 5 years old." Deaths rose from 4.6 million (2024) to 4.8 million (2025) — the first increase this century, driven by reduced aid from rich countries to poor countries.

Critical stat: "If funding for health decreases by 20 percent, 12 million more children could die by 2045."

## Three Key Questions

### 1. Will a world that is getting richer increase its generosity toward those in need?

- Aid represented less than 1% of GDP even in the most generous countries
- Record number of billionaires and centibillionaires — philanthropy should grow
- Gates will spend much of his year advocating for increased health funding
- The Giving Pledge as a model for philanthropic commitment
- Goalkeepers report details what's at risk

### 2. Will the world prioritize scaling innovations that improve equality?

Three critical areas:

**Climate:**
- Without a global carbon tax (politically unachievable), market forces won't incentivize emissions reduction
- World has cut projected emissions by 40%+ in the last decade
- Still need innovation in industrial emissions and aviation
- Climate adaptation: AI will give poor farmers better weather/price/disease advice than rich farmers get
- Gates Foundation committed $1.4B to supporting farmers on climate frontlines
- Gates will invest and give "more than ever" to climate work

**Healthcare:**
- Alzheimer's diagnosis breakthrough will "revolutionize how we test for—and ultimately prevent—this disease"
- AI to accelerate development AND delivery of healthcare
- Gates already uses AI to understand his own health
- Developers still working on reliability and connecting AI to doctors/nurses
- Gates Foundation + OpenAI: Horizon1000 initiative — $50M for AI in African healthcare
- Rwanda: 1 health worker per 1,000 people (WHO recommends ~4), 180-year gap at current pace
- Minister of Health Dr. Sabin Nsanzimana launched AI-powered Health Intelligence Center in Kigali
- Goal: reach 1,000 primary healthcare clinics by 2028
- Ensuring uncommon African languages are fully supported

**Education:**
- AI enables personalized learning to keep students motivated
- Now a focus of Gates Foundation education spending
- Gates has seen results firsthand in New Jersey

### 3. Will we minimize negative disruptions caused by AI?

Key claims:
- "Of all the things humans have ever created, AI will change society the most"
- "There is no upper limit on how intelligent AIs will get or on how good robots will get, and I believe the advances will not plateau before exceeding human levels"
- When AGI/robot predictions are missed, people assume they'll never happen — but they will

Two big challenges in the next decade:
1. **Bad actors:** Bioterrorism risk from open-source AI tools — "an even greater risk than a naturally caused pandemic"
2. **Job market disruption:** AI makes software devs "at least twice as efficient." Job disruption already starting and will grow over next 5 years. Need policies to spread wealth and address the role jobs play in society.

On computing economics: "Computing is a good historical example where lower costs actually caused the overall market to grow."

## Closing

Gates' optimism rests on two human capabilities: (1) ability to anticipate problems and prepare for them, (2) capacity to care about each other. "Foresight and care" give him hope.
