# Brain-Computer Interface

A brain-computer interface (BCI) is a system that translates neural signals from the brain into commands for external devices -- robotic limbs, cursors, speech synthesizers, or other outputs.

## How It Works
- Electrodes (implanted or surface-level) record electrical activity from neurons
- Signal processing algorithms decode intent from neural patterns
- Decoded intent drives an external device (e.g., robotic hand, computer cursor, speech output)

## Current State (as of March 2026)
- **China:** First country to approve a BCI for use outside clinical trials -- a chip that lets paralysis patients control a soft robotic hand ([[china-approves-brain-chip]])
- **Neuralink (US):** [[neuralink]] conducting human trials; demonstrated thought-to-text and cursor control
- Applications expanding beyond paralysis: speech restoration, expression, singing

## Key Players
- [[neuralink]] -- Elon Musk's company, highest-profile US effort
- Chinese research teams (unnamed in source) -- first to regulatory approval
- BrainGate -- long-running US academic BCI program
