# Wayloft Three-Layer Funnel Design

## Key Takeaways
- Core problem: Wayloft is fully built and deployed but has **zero users**. Distribution, not features.
- Demand evidence is thin. One named user: Annabel's dad. Peers expressed confusion but no formal validation.
- "The gap between the founder's enthusiasm (transfer bonuses are cool!) and the named user's actual need (help me see what I have) is the single biggest risk."
- Transfer bonuses as the wedge is wrong — audience tiny, already served by Frequent Miler. AF decision helper is a better entry point.
- Three-Layer Funnel:
  - **Layer 1:** AI Persona ([[ellis-church]]) + SEO content. Card reviews, "Is Your Card Worth It?" verdicts, best-of guides. Blocked on persona design.
  - **Layer 1.5:** Browser extension. "Which Card Right Now" daily habit. No login required.
  - **Layer 2:** Personalized newsletter via [[beehiiv]]. Custom fields per bank. Welcome sequence.
  - **Layer 3:** Full portfolio app (already built, no changes needed).
- [[worth-it-tool]] spec: `/credit-cards/[slug]/worth-it`, no auth, toggle benefits, keep/call/downgrade verdict, shareable OG card
- Affiliate rejection contingency: fall back to Amazon Associates + Skimlinks
- Annabel starts at Okta after graduation — Wayloft becomes side project
- No budget for paid acquisition — organic only
- The Assignment: "No new backend features for the next 4 weeks. Everything you build should be content, distribution, or extracting existing features into public-facing tools."

## Success Criteria
- 30 days: persona created, 10 reviews refreshed, tool live, first Reddit post, newsletter signup, MVP extension
- 90 days: 500 newsletter, 100 web app signups, 200+ extension installs, $50/mo affiliate
- 6 months: 2,000 newsletter, 500 active users, 1,000+ extension, $500/mo
- 12 months: 10,000 newsletter, 2,000 active users, 5,000+ extension, $2,000/mo
