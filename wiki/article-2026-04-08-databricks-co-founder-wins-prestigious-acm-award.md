# Databricks Co-Founder Matei Zaharia Wins 2026 ACM Prize in Computing

## Key Takeaways
- [[matei-zaharia]] — Databricks co-founder and CTO — won the 2026 ACM Prize in Computing ($250K cash prize, being donated to charity).
- The award cites his collective contributions, rooted in the 2009 UC Berkeley PhD work (under [[ion-stoica]]) that became [[apache-spark]].
- Spark was the "big data was the new AI" moment of 2009 — dramatically sped up slow big-data pipelines and made Zaharia a tech celebrity at 28.
- [[databricks]] has since raised $20B+, is valued at ~$134B, and hit $5.4B revenue. Zaharia still runs engineering and is also an associate professor at Berkeley.
- Zaharia's AGI claim: **"AGI is here already. It's just not in a form that we appreciate."** His argument: stop applying human standards to AI models — an AI that passes the bar doesn't have human-grade general knowledge, because ingesting facts is trivial for it.
- Zaharia on [[open-claw]]: "On the one hand, it's awesome... but it's also a security nightmare" because it mimics a human assistant you trust with passwords, so a compromised agent could spend unauthorized money from a logged-in browser.
- Where he's most excited: **AI for research/engineering search** — accurate, no-hallucination research. Analogy: vibe coding made programming accessible; research AI will make understanding information universal. "Not that many people need to build applications, but lots of people need to understand information."

## Notable Claims
- **AGI framing:** The anthropomorphizing of AI is the central mistake. Applying human benchmarks (bar exam, etc.) misreads what these systems are and are not.
- **Agent security:** The design choice that makes browser-based agents useful (inheriting your logged-in state, mimicking you) is the exact thing that makes them a vulnerability. This is a direct professional validation of the concern Annabel already flagged in [[open-claw]].
- **Research is the killer app:** Not app-building. The larger market is people who need to *understand* information — car diagnostics, cross-modal sensing (radio/microwaves beyond text/image), molecular simulation for biology.
