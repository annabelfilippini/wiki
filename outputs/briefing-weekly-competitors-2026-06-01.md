# Wayloft Competitor Brief — 2026-06-01

## Top 5

1. **CardPointers: Biggest upgrade in app history (v7) — plus a 50% sale** — **Threat.** CardPointers v7 launched this cycle with a full Liquid Glass visual redesign, on-device Apple Intelligence integration ("Siri, ask CardPointers"), Apple Watch nearby-store lookup, CarPlay support, custom card creation, and an Android v7 beta that finally closes the iOS-only gap. Simultaneously running a 50% off sale. This is Wayloft's closest direct competitor — portfolio optimization, card-by-card guidance, spending category routing — and they just had their most significant product moment ever. The sale will convert fence-sitters. ([CardPointers v7 review](https://cloud9club.net/card-pointers-review/), [AppAdvice](https://appadvice.com/post/cardpointers-7-is-here-with-liquid-glass-redesign-and-much-more/782394))

2. **Bilt 2.0 backlash = live SEO demand for Wayloft's Worth-It tool** — **Opportunity.** Bilt launched three new cards in January 2026 (no-fee, $95, $495) with a second rewards currency (Bilt Cash), sparking the "most complicated rewards system we've ever seen" headline from the NYT. NerdWallet published "Bilt 2.0 Promises Rewards, Delivers Confusion." Users spent "hours of back-of-the-napkin calculations" trying to determine if staying on Bilt is worth it at all. This is the exact problem Wayloft's Worth-It tool solves — and the search intent is live right now. ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/bilt-promises-rewards-delivers-confusion), [Thrifty Traveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/news/credit-card/bilt-2-0-backlash-explained/))

3. **Capital One launches dedicated Travel App (March 2026)** — **Threat/Neutral.** Capital One's new standalone app (live March 17) consolidates booking (flights, hotels, cars, activities), miles redemption, lounge access with a Live Lounge Locator, and real-time trip management — all inside one issuer-native product. The key constraint: it only works with Capital One cards. It won't pull in Chase UR or Amex MR. This is a threat because it normalizes "one app for your card perks" as a concept, but it's a siloed walled-garden, not a cross-issuer optimizer. Wayloft's cross-issuer portfolio view remains differentiated. ([Capital One newsroom](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-launches-capital-one-travel-app/), [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/news/capital-one-travel-app))

4. **Point.me launches events.point.me World Cup hub (May 14, 2026)** — **Opportunity.** Point.me pivoted to build a dedicated travel hub for World Cup 2026 (USA/Canada/Mexico), integrating award flight search across 150+ programs with match ticket purchasing via FrequentFan. Search speed improved ~30% (initial results in ~7 seconds). Point.me is moving up the funnel — they're not just "find award seats," they're now a full trip planner around live events. This signals that event-based demand spikes are the next frontier for award search. Wayloft doesn't compete here directly, but it confirms that contextual hooks (World Cup, card renewal season, Bilt launch confusion) drive traffic spikes. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/pointme-launches-travel-hub-to-help-fans-find-award-flights-and-experiences-for-soccers-biggest-event-302771838.html))

5. **Seats.aero adds Seat Map Viewer + Fare Class Viewer (Pro, 2026)** — **Neutral.** Seats.aero added a Seat Map Viewer (beta, Pro at $9.99/mo) plus a Fare Class Viewer, JetBlue Move-to-Mint upgrade finder, and expanded programs (Azul, Ethiopian, Saudia). AwardFares countered with free seat maps across its Gold ($9.99) and Diamond ($19.99) tiers. Both tools are deepening in the award search lane — not Wayloft's card optimization lane — but they set user expectations for what $9.99/mo buys. Wayloft Pro at $9.99/mo will be compared to Seats.aero Pro in user mental models. ([AwardFares](https://awardfares.com/blog/awardfares-vs-seats-aero/), [Seats.aero](https://seats.aero/))

## User Pain Points

- **Bilt 2.0 math fatigue:** Users doing hours of manual calculations to determine if their Bilt card is still worth keeping post-restructure. Two rewards currencies, unclear stacking rules, declined credit limits at launch. Direct demand for a "just tell me if it's worth it" tool.
- **Rewards program complexity creeping everywhere:** NerdWallet called out "cards with confusing rewards programs" as a category. Bank of America's new BofA Rewards program (launched May 27) is another complexity event — existing cardholders may see reduced bonuses without understanding why.
- **No single tool tracks cross-issuer perks holistically:** CardPointers and MaxRewards both optimize at the transaction level ("which card do I tap right now"). Neither gives a clean annual portfolio view — what your effective annual fee is after netting out statement credits, travel credits, and perks actually used.
- **Award search is fast, but decision support is missing:** Seats.aero/AwardFares find the availability; nothing tells you whether those points are better burned on a flight vs. kept for a transfer bonus. Wayloft's CPP-weighted optimization fills this gap but isn't yet user-visible.

## Watch List

- **TPG expanding app into portfolio management:** TPG's mobile app now includes a Cards tab analyzing spending and tracking bonus progress, and a Points tab with loyalty balances. TPG has 10M+ monthly readers — if they push the app hard, they become a distribution threat, not just a content one.
- **AI integration in card tools accelerating:** CardPointers is shipping on-device Apple Intelligence. MaxRewards has "best card right now" AI routing. The expectation is shifting — a card tool without AI feels dated. Ellis Church as the Wayloft voice is the right strategic response, but needs to ship publicly.
- **Gondola entering the email-connected balance tracker space:** Gondola identifies accumulated points by connecting to email, estimates value, and tracks expiration. Early but overlaps with Wayloft's balance awareness positioning. Monitor.
- **Issuer-native apps as walled gardens:** Capital One's move follows a broader trend where Chase (Sapphire app), Amex (benefits tracker), and now Capital One are all building in-house tools. They have cardholder data advantages no third party can match — but they'll never cover the full portfolio.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Write Bilt 2.0 Worth-It content this week.** Search intent for "is Bilt worth it," "is Bilt Obsidian worth the annual fee," and "Bilt 2.0 explained" is live and growing. Wayloft's Worth-It tool is purpose-built for this moment. Write three SSG pages: the Bilt Obsidian ($495), Bilt Palladium ($95), and the no-fee card. Lead with Ellis Church's voice. This is the clearest distribution play available right now.

2. **Run a complexity-as-the-enemy content angle.** Bilt 2.0 backlash, BofA Rewards restructure, and general rewards complexity creep are all in the news. A piece titled "Your credit card rewards shouldn't require a spreadsheet" — published by Ellis Church — directly addresses current sentiment and positions Wayloft's annual fee decision helper as the antidote. This is top-of-funnel content that converts to the Worth-It tool.

3. **Don't match CardPointers on transaction-level features.** CardPointers v7 with Apple Intelligence on Watch is the best "which card do I tap" experience ever shipped. Wayloft can't win that fight solo. Instead, own the layer above — the annual portfolio decision — which neither CardPointers nor MaxRewards does well. The Worth-It tool is already there; double down on content that surfaces it.

4. **Benchmark Wayloft Pro's $9.99 value clearly in launch messaging.** With Seats.aero at $9.99 and AwardFares Gold at $9.99, users have a mental anchor for what $9.99/mo buys in this space. Wayloft Pro's value story needs to be crisp and distinct: "Seats.aero tells you where to fly. Wayloft tells you if your cards are worth keeping."

5. **Stay the course on flight search deferral.** Capital One's all-in booking app launch and Point.me's World Cup hub both confirm that award booking is increasingly crowded and capital-intensive. Wayloft's Duffel live access block is actually fine — the card optimization lane is less competitive and more defensible for a solo founder.
