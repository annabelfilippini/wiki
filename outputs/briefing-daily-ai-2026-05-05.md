# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-05

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Agentic AI hits default mode** — May 2026 is the inflection point everyone will look back on. Microsoft Agent 365 launched May 1 as a $15/user/month enterprise governance layer for AI agents; Devin is autonomously handling full-repo coding with tests and PRs; and the Lovable/Bolt/Replit stack has created a vibe-to-production pipeline where indie builders ship full apps in hours with near-zero manual code. The category has shifted: from "AI helps you work" to "AI does the work." Real practitioners on HN and r/SideProject are shipping products they couldn't have built solo a year ago. ([Product Hunt AI Agents](https://www.producthunt.com/categories/ai-agents))

2. **Claude Opus 4.7 earning its keep on hard tasks** — Anthropic's mid-April release is now in practitioners' hands and the benchmark numbers are holding up in real use. 13% coding benchmark lift over Opus 4.6, including four tasks that stumped both Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6 entirely. High-resolution vision (2576px / 3.75MP) is the sleeper feature — useful for architecture diagrams, UI screenshot analysis, and document parsing. Notable wrinkle: all cybersecurity uses are blocked by default; legitimate security work now requires applying to Anthropic's new Cyber Verification Program, which is generating friction and debate in security circles. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-7))

3. **Gemini Deep Research Agent goes GA** — Google's autonomous research agent is now generally available. Early reports on r/MachineLearning position it as genuinely useful for multi-step synthesis tasks — not just search-and-summarize, but structured analysis across long document sets. Weak spots: proprietary data, real-time context, and anything requiring live web access. The strongest use case emerging is literature review and competitive analysis — exactly the kind of tedious multi-source work that practitioners have wanted to offload for years. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-22/google-releases-new-ai-agents-to-challenge-openai-and-anthropic))

4. **2026 is shaping up as the Year of AI-Assisted Cyberattacks** — Security researchers are documenting a measurable uptick in AI-generated phishing, exploit code, and social engineering campaigns. The concern isn't superintelligent hacking; it's cheap, scalable, personalized attacks that previously required skilled operators now being accessible to script kiddies. Anthropic's Project Glasswing and the Opus 4.7 Cyber Verification Program are explicit responses to this. Expect this thread to intensify — every major AI lab is quietly wrestling with dual-use, and the policy window is closing. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/2026-year-of-ai-assisted-attacks.html))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Pentagon locks out Anthropic — and Anthropic sues** — The DoD finalized classified AI agreements for IL6/IL7 networks with eight companies: OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI. Anthropic was explicitly excluded. The dispute: Anthropic refused to remove safety guardrails that would allow Claude to be used for autonomous weapons systems and mass surveillance under "all lawful purposes" terms. The Trump administration responded by blacklisting the company entirely. Anthropic sued; a California federal judge blocked the ban; the DC Circuit upheld it. This is the first major public fight between an AI safety-first company and the US military over terms-of-use — and it draws a clean line between labs willing to go to classified weapons applications and those that aren't. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/01/tech/pentagon-ai-anthropic)) ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/05/01/pentagon-ai-deals-microsoft-amazon-google-classified-military/))

2. **OpenAI breaks the Microsoft exclusivity moat** — GPT-5.5 is out as OpenAI's strongest model yet, with real gains in coding, computer use, and agent-style workflows. The bigger story is structural: OpenAI loosened its Microsoft cloud exclusivity arrangement and is now distributing through Amazon, with a path to Google too. Two years ago Azure was OpenAI's distribution moat; the new posture says OpenAI believes it can win on product merit alone. The move also signals OpenAI's increasing confidence that no single cloud partnership is necessary — it can play the field. ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news))

3. **The realization that's actually landing: AI is replacing white-collar work, not augmenting it** — Goldman Sachs has 16K US white-collar job losses per month attributed to AI. Meta's 8K layoffs starting May 20 are explicitly AI-linked in internal communications. The Q1 earnings season made the pattern undeniable: tech companies are reporting AI productivity gains and headcount cuts in the same sentence, same slide. The narrative has shifted from "AI will create new jobs to replace the old ones" to "AI is eliminating a predictable category of work, and the replacement timeline is 18–24 months, not a decade." The May Day protests this year had a visible AI displacement thread that didn't exist last year. ([Goldman Sachs via Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-22/google-releases-new-ai-agents-to-challenge-openai-and-anthropic)) ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates))

## World News

1. **Iran attacks UAE; US sinks Iranian boats in Hormuz** — The UAE's air defenses intercepted 15 Iranian missiles and 4 drones on Monday, with one drone sparking a fire at a Fujairah oil facility and wounding three workers. The US sank 7 small Iranian boats attempting to interfere with shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump announced "Project Freedom" — a US military escort operation to guide stranded commercial vessels through the strait — and warned Iran would be "blown off the face of the Earth" if US Navy ships are attacked. Iran called the US presence a ceasefire violation. This marks a significant escalation from last week's diplomatic track; the ceasefire Trump announced as extended on April 21 is now functionally in peril. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/5/4/iran-war-live-tehran-says-trumps-hormuz-mission-violates-ceasefire)) ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/04/nx-s1-5810508/iran-war-updates)) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/04/iran-war-uae-trump-ceasefire-missiles.html))

2. **Russia and Ukraine declare competing, non-coordinated ceasefires** — Both sides announced separate unilateral ceasefires timed around Russia's Victory Day (May 8–9). Putin proposed his ceasefire via a phone call with Trump; Zelenskyy separately announced Ukraine would halt fire starting midnight May 5–6, saying "human life is incomparably more valuable than the celebration of any anniversary." Neither side acknowledged the other's terms, and no joint mechanism exists for enforcement. A Russian missile strike on Kharkiv killed 7 civilians hours after the announcements. The competing declarations underscore how far apart the parties remain despite the diplomatic optics. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/4/russia-and-ukraine-declare-competing-ceasefires)) ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/05/g-s1-120129/russia-declares-a-truce-in-ukraine-to-mark-victory-day)) ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/05/04/russia-unilaterally-declares-victory-day-ceasefire-while-zelenskyy-tables-own-truce))

3. **Fed analysis: tariffs cost US economy ~19,000 jobs per month** — A Federal Reserve analysis published this week found that the current US tariff regime has suppressed job growth by an estimated 19,000 positions per month, with manufacturers down 88,000 jobs year-over-year. The average annual cost to a US household is estimated at $1,500. The findings come after the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in February that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs — only Section 232 tariffs remain legally intact. Economists note the full pass-through to consumer prices is still arriving, with Goldman Sachs projecting a 1% inflation increase through mid-2026. ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/tariffs-may-have-cost-us-economy-thousands-jobs-monthly-fed-analysis-reveals)) ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/29/tariffs-not-strengthening-economy-trump-trade-war-data/))

4. **Hantavirus outbreak kills 3 on cruise ship off Cape Verde** — A cruise ship carrying nearly 150 passengers is waiting for emergency assistance off the coast of Cape Verde after three passengers died in a suspected hantavirus outbreak. Hantavirus is rare, not typically spread person-to-person, and has no approved antiviral treatment — making the cause of a shipboard cluster unusual and under investigation. Authorities are treating it as a potential public health incident. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-trump-strait-of-hormuz-ship-attack-threat-peace-proposal/))
