# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-01

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Microsoft 365 E7 "Frontier Suite" is live today** — Microsoft's first new enterprise license tier since E5 launched in 2015 goes generally available today at $99/user/month. It bundles E5, Copilot, Agent 365, and Entra Suite — 15% cheaper than buying them separately. The centerpiece is Agent 365, a control plane that lets IT departments manage, audit, and govern AI agents running across their org at scale. Built in collaboration with Anthropic (Claude under the hood). Enterprise AI governance is now a product category with a SKU. ([Trustmarque](https://trustmarque.com/microsoft-365-e7-agent-365-whats-launching-1-may-and-what-it-means))

2. **Anthropic Mythos found and fixed 271 Firefox vulnerabilities** — Mozilla ran Anthropic's restricted Mythos model against the Firefox codebase and it surfaced 271 security bugs, all of which were patched in Firefox 150. This is the most concrete public demonstration yet of what Project Glasswing actually looks like in practice: one model pass, 271 real browser fixes that human security researchers hadn't caught. Mythos remains unavailable to the public — distributed only to the 40+ organizations in the Glasswing consortium. ([Anthropic](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/mythos-preview/))

3. **Perplexity Comet hits enterprise** — Comet, Perplexity's AI-native Chromium browser where the AI is the core interaction model (not a sidebar), is now available for enterprise orgs with MDM deployment across macOS and Windows. Enterprise admins can push it silently fleet-wide; Max subscribers can select Claude Opus 4.6 as the underlying model. The browser went from iOS launch (March) to enterprise rollout in under 6 weeks — fast product velocity. The idea: your browser is your agent, not just your tab manager. ([Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/comet-is-now-available-to-everyone-worldwide))

4. **App releases up 104% YoY — AI is the reason** — TechCrunch analysis finds worldwide app releases in Q1 2026 were up 60% YoY, and April specifically hit +104% versus the same month last year. Lovable (describe what you want → full-stack app generated) hit $20M ARR in its first two months — fastest ramp of any AI app builder ever. The pattern: AI tools that compress the path from idea to deployed product are triggering a new wave of solo and small-team entrepreneurship. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Anthropic is closing a $50B round at a ~$900B valuation** — The round is expected to close within two weeks, with an IPO floated for as early as October. For context: Anthropic was at roughly $9B ARR at the end of 2025. It's now above $30B ARR. Google's $40B investment (announced April 24 — $10B upfront, up to $30B contingent on milestones, plus 5 gigawatts of dedicated compute) is the anchor of this round. The implied valuation compression from startup to near-trillion-dollar company in under a year is without precedent. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40-billion-in-anthropic-as-search-giant-spreads-its-ai-bets.html))

2. **The OpenAI-Microsoft post-exclusivity era starts today** — The amended partnership announced April 27 takes full effect: OpenAI can now serve products on any cloud (clearing the path for its $50B Amazon deal), Microsoft remains "primary" partner through 2032 but exclusivity is gone. Microsoft no longer owes OpenAI a revenue share; OpenAI's payments to Microsoft continue through 2030. The so-what: cloud AI exclusivity as a moat is officially dead. The competition moves to agent runtimes and application layers, where Microsoft is betting E7 can win enterprise. ([Microsoft Blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/04/27/the-next-phase-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/openai-microsoft-partnership-revenue-cap.html))

3. **Huawei's Ascend 950PR is doing real numbers** — Huawei expects AI chip revenue to hit ~$12B in 2026, up 60% from $7.5B in 2025, as orders for the Ascend 950PR surge. The US chip embargo — designed to keep China from building competitive AI infrastructure — hasn't stopped this. China is running a parallel AI hardware track, and Huawei is its primary vehicle. Broader implication: two non-interoperable AI hardware ecosystems are solidifying, with long-term consequences for model availability, training, and deployment across different markets.

4. **The cybersecurity AI shift is real and accelerating** — The Mythos/Firefox story is a signal of a broader change: AI is moving from "helps developers write safer code" to "actively scans and patches existing systems at scale." OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are working together to block Chinese competitors from extracting frontier model weights, while Project Glasswing is using those same models to harden critical infrastructure. Security is becoming the highest-stakes AI application category. ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/04/07/tech/openai-anthropic-google-china-copy/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-mythos-preview-cybersecurity-risks))

## World News

1. **May Day Strong strikes hit today across the US** — More than 500 unions, student groups, and community organizations called for a nationwide "no work, no school, no shopping" day on May 1, protesting Trump administration policies on immigration, taxation, and what organizers describe as a "billionaire takeover of government." Twenty-plus North Carolina school districts closed due to planned staff absences; Chicago secured an official "day of civic action" designation for teachers. Organizers (including NEA and the Chicago Teachers Union) expected over 100,000 students to miss school and over 3,500 actions nationwide. The protests have drawn both support from labor groups and sharp criticism from conservatives who say they disrupt education. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/01/nx-s1-5805805/may-day-protests-boycott-schools-trump), [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/may-day-protests-take-place-friday-agitators-across-us-push-workers-billionaires-motto))

2. **Ukraine: Putin signals May 9 ceasefire, Tuapse hit again** — Russian President Putin indicated Russia is prepared to declare a ceasefire on May 9 (Victory Day). Trump and Zelensky have reportedly agreed on 90-95% of a peace framework, though Zelensky has approved new offensive operations in parallel. Ukraine struck Russia's Tuapse oil refinery for the fourth time, triggering a state of emergency in the city; Ukrainian drones also destroyed a Rostov-on-Don FPV drone warehouse holding ~6,000 units. The pattern: diplomacy and escalation are running simultaneously, with neither side willing to stop striking until a deal is signed. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/30/its-all-very-toxic), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-29/russia-cuts-tanks-missiles-from-putin-s-wwii-victory-parade-in-first-since-2007))

3. **Iran: talks stalled, crude storage may run out in under three weeks** — US-Iran diplomatic talks remain deadlocked. The US military blockade of Iranian ports (launched April 13) and Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz (February 28, when the US-Israel air war began) are both still in effect. Bloomberg/Kpler analysis suggests Iran could exhaust its crude storage capacity in 12-22 days if the blockade persists, potentially forcing a production halt. Tehran is losing ~$500M/day. US gas prices are up nearly 30 cents in a single week. One-fifth of global oil and LNG normally transits the strait. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/28/as-us-iran-talks-remain-stalled-experts-warn-of-long-term-disruptions), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/30/trump-blockade-iran-oil-strait-hormuz.html))

4. **Press freedom hits a 25-year low globally** — The Reporters Without Borders (RSF) World Press Freedom Index, published yesterday, finds press freedom at its worst since the index began in 2002. More than half of the 180 countries surveyed (52.2%) are now rated "difficult" or "very serious" — up from 13.7% in 2002. Only 1% of the world's population lives in a country with "good" press freedom (down from 20% in 2002); only seven northern European nations qualify. The US fell to 64th. The steepest single-year drop: Niger (-37 places). Biggest improvement: post-Assad Syria. The legal indicator declined the most, reflecting criminalization of journalism as the primary tool of suppression. ([RSF](https://rsf.org/en/2026-rsf-index-press-freedom-25-year-low), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/30/global-press-freedom-falls-to-its-lowest-level-in-25-years))
