# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-11

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Karpathy Declares Vibe Coding "Passé," Names the New Paradigm** — Speaking at AI Ascent 2026, Karpathy said LLMs are now powerful enough that one-shot vibe prompting is the floor, not the ceiling — and coined a new term for what comes next: "agentic engineering." The distinction matters: vibe coding is a personal productivity hack, agentic engineering is a production methodology where agents plan, execute, check outcomes, and iterate with limited human involvement between steps. The framing is circulating fast on X because it names what senior developers are already living — the shift from "write code for me" to "orchestrate systems that write code." His descriptions land as near-term workflow signals; expect teams to start using this language immediately. ([Source](https://aintelligencehub.com/articles/karpathy-vibe-coding-to-agent-workflows-may-2026))

2. **Claude Opus 4.7 Ships + Rate Limits Double** — Anthropic released Opus 4.7 this week with measurable gains on hard software engineering tasks and higher-resolution vision. Same week: Anthropic signed a deal with SpaceX to use all of Colossus 1 (300MW, 220K+ NVIDIA GPUs), enabling them to double rate limits across Pro/Max/Team/Enterprise tiers and eliminate peak-hour throttling. Also new: Claude Security entered public beta for Enterprise — automated code vulnerability scanning with Opus 4.7 doing the analysis and proposing fixes. For anyone using Claude Code daily, this week is a tangible upgrade on all three axes: capability, throughput, and security tooling. ([Source](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-7))

3. **Lovable: $300M ARR, Under One Year Old** — Lovable hit $300M annualized revenue by January 2026, less than 12 months after launch. Cursor is at $2B ARR. The "vibe coding" era has minted enormous businesses by lowering the floor on who can build software. What's coming into focus as the next question (per Karpathy's point above) is whether these tools successfully make the leap to agentic production workflows or stay primarily prototyping tools. That transition — or failure to make it — is what will determine which tools lead the next cycle. ([Source](https://manus.im/blog/best-vibe-coding-tools))

4. **Google I/O Preview: Gemini 4, Ironwood TPUs, AI Glasses — One Week Out** — Google I/O is May 19 (8 days away), and specs are leaking: Gemini 4, Ironwood TPUs running at 42.5 exaflops per chip (roughly 20× the H100), AI glasses, Android 17 with Gemini deep integration, and a Gemini Deep Research Agent for autonomous research across web and private sources with multimodal output. The compute numbers alone are a statement — Google is positioning Ironwood as infrastructure dominance, not just model competitiveness. Watch for the Gemini Deep Research Agent as the direct NotebookLM/ChatGPT Deep Research competitor. ([Source](https://www.mejba.me/blog/google-io-2026-ai-announcements))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Anthropic Hits $30B Run Rate, Launches Enterprise Consulting JV** — Anthropic's annualized revenue surpassed $30B — up from ~$9B at end of 2025 — making it the fastest-growing software company on record. Alongside this, Anthropic announced a new enterprise AI services company structured with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs, moving directly into the consulting and implementation space. This mirrors OpenAI's simultaneous move the same week (covered May 8). The pattern: AI labs are no longer content to sell API access while McKinsey captures the margin on enterprise transformation. They want the implementation fees too. ([Source](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute))

2. **US Government Formalizes AI Pre-Deployment Testing** — CAISI (Center for AI Standards and Innovation) announced agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI to evaluate their models before public release — joining OpenAI and Anthropic, who signed earlier. The Trump administration now has pre-clearance relationships with all five major frontier labs. This is still voluntary, but the infrastructure for mandatory federal AI clearance is being quietly assembled. Expect this to become a formal regulatory mechanism within 12–18 months. ([Source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/05/ai-oversight-trump-google-microsoft-xai.html))

3. **61% of Engineering Teams Now Running Agents in Production** — Anthropic's 2026 Agentic Coding Trends Report dropped this week: 61% of engineering teams are running AI agents (not assistants — full autonomous loops) in production. Agents are handling entire feature branches without human involvement in the execution loop. The cultural implication is now unavoidable: if agents are doing the build, what are software engineers for? The emerging consensus is "taste, governance, and orchestration" — but that answer is contested, and the discourse is just beginning to shift from capability celebration to labor reckoning. ([Source](https://resources.anthropic.com/hubfs/2026%20Agentic%20Coding%20Trends%20Report.pdf))

## World News

1. **Ukraine Ceasefire Final Day: Violations Mount, Putin Hints at Peace** — Today is the last day of Trump's brokered 3-day Russia-Ukraine ceasefire (May 9–11). Ukraine reports ~150 combat engagements and at least 3 dead since it took effect — both sides are accusing the other of violations. At the same time, Putin publicly suggested the war may be "coming to an end" and expressed willingness to meet Zelenskyy in a third country. Analysts are divided: some see genuine war-weariness; others note that Putin has floated peace signals repeatedly across 18 months without following through. A durable peace requires terms neither side has publicly agreed to. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/10/putin-suggests-russias-war-on-ukraine-coming-to-an-end)) ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/russia-ukraine-ceasefire-9.7193061))

2. **US-China Tariff Truce: 145% → 30%, 90-Day Clock Starts** — Following weekend talks in Geneva, the US and China agreed to a 90-day tariff truce: US tariffs on Chinese goods drop from 145% to 30%; China's tariffs on US goods drop from 125% to 10%. Global markets rallied on the news. The deal represents real de-escalation from the most extreme point of the trade war, but 30% remains historically high by post-WWII norms. The 90-day window restarts full negotiations; a permanent deal remains far out. Both sides framed this as a tactical pause. ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/us-china-trade-truce-donald-trump-xi-jinping-apec/804233/)) ([Coface](https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/us-china-trade-agreement-a-tactical-truce-not-a-strategic-shift))

3. **Strait of Hormuz: Military Escalation and Diplomacy Running in Parallel** — The US struck two Iranian-flagged tankers last week; Iran seized a US-affiliated vessel in retaliation; an estimated 1,600 ships remain stranded or rerouted. Simultaneously, US-Iran nuclear talks are reported as "close to a 14-point MOU" that would include an enrichment moratorium and reopening of the Strait. These two tracks — active military strikes and diplomatic progress — are running in parallel, creating an unusually unpredictable risk environment for global energy supply. No ceasefire or de-escalation agreement has been announced as of this morning. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/5/7/us-trade-court-rules-against-trumps-10-percent-global-tariffs))

4. **India-Pakistan: One Year After Operation Sindoor, Escalation Risks Return** — May marks the one-year anniversary of the 88-hour India-Pakistan air war (Operation Sindoor, May 7–10, 2025). Both governments are publicly claiming victory and rearming. The ceasefire holds in practice, but India has not reinstated the Indus Waters Treaty, which governs 80%+ of Pakistan's agricultural water (240M people dependent). Pakistan's military issued a formal escalation warning this week. The water dispute is now the highest-stakes unresolved issue between the two nuclear-armed states — more durable than border skirmishes, and harder to negotiate. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/10/two-wins-two-losses-what-india-pakistan-have-learned-a-year-after-war)) ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2026/05/05/year-later-india-pakistan-ceasefire-is-holding-so-far/))
