# Wayloft Competitor Brief — 2026-06-15

## Top 5

1. **CardPointers v7: ChatGPT + Apple Intelligence Integration** — **Threat.** CardPointers shipped its largest redesign ever this cycle. iOS gets ChatGPT integration for natural-language portfolio questions ("Ask AI about my cards") and on-device Apple Intelligence via Siri. Android gets a full Material Expressive redesign with custom card support and major performance improvements. Vision Pro and Mac are also supported. This directly attacks Ellis Church's differentiation: CardPointers now offers the same "ask AI about your portfolio" experience Wayloft is building toward. The key counter: CardPointers' AI is a double-paywall (CardPointers subscription + ChatGPT Plus), while Ellis Church is native. But CardPointers' 50% sale ($45/yr) is reportedly still live, and they have 800K+ users vs. Wayloft's zero — the incumbent AI pivot is dangerous at launch stage. Wayloft response: sharpen the "native intelligence vs. AI plumbing" positioning immediately. ([Source](https://appadvice.com/post/cardpointers-7-is-here-with-liquid-glass-redesign-and-much-more/782394))

2. **Chase Sapphire Reserve $795 Fee — SEO Spike Still Running** — **Opportunity.** Chase raised the CSR annual fee from $550 to $795 in June 2025 (a 45% increase). One year later, the "is the CSR worth it in 2026" content wave is still cresting: NerdWallet, Motley Fool, AFAR, and Upgraded Points all have active 2026 pieces ranking for "Chase Sapphire Reserve worth the annual fee." This is exactly what Wayloft's worth-it tool computes. The CSR SSG page — which should be the highest-traffic worth-it page in Wayloft's 54-card lineup — needs to be indexed now. Search intent is high, competition is established content sites (not tool pages), and Wayloft's interactive verdict format is genuinely differentiated. Wayloft response: ship CSR worth-it SSG page and Ellis Church analysis this week, targeting "[Chase Sapphire Reserve] worth it 2026" and "is the CSR worth $795." ([Source](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/is-chase-sapphire-reserve-worth-its-annual-fee))

3. **NerdWallet Doubles Down on PointsYeah as #1 Award Tool** — **Structural Threat.** NerdWallet has published a full review declaring PointsYeah the best award travel search tool in 2026: 22 airline programs, 6 hotel programs, Daydream Explorer for inspiration-based searching, seat maps, free tier + $99.99/yr premium. NerdWallet + TPG + Bankrate collectively own 62%+ of AI assistant citations in the travel rewards space (per prior scan). As these outlets increasingly route travelers to PointsYeah for award search, they consolidate their category authority and leave less SEO oxygen for new entrants. PointsYeah is not in Wayloft's lane (award search vs. card-decision), but NerdWallet's growing authority over the entire rewards query space is a structural headwind for Wayloft's SEO strategy. Wayloft response: invest in card-decision query types NerdWallet doesn't own — "[card] worth it after fee increase," "[card] annual fee calculator," "should I cancel [card]" — rather than fighting on award-search terms. ([Source](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/points-yeah-award-search-review-easily-find-your-next-points-redemption))

4. **Gondola Adds Price Alerts** — **Neutral / Opportunity.** Gondola (AI hotel booking platform, free, $3M seed) launched price-drop alert functionality for hotel bookings. It stacks hotel loyalty points + credit card points + 3-7% Gondola Cash on every booking and now alerts users to price drops. The 2026 status challenge offers 5% cashback for Silver members who book 5 nights. Gondola is solidifying the "hotel points optimizer" category adjacent to Wayloft. No direct competition (Wayloft is card-decision, Gondola is hotel-booking), but Gondola's growth validates that points-stacking complexity is a real user problem. Wayloft can reference Gondola as a complementary tool in hotel spend content ("use Gondola for hotel stacking, Wayloft to decide if your card is worth keeping") — drives topical authority and potential cross-referrals without building a competing product. ([Source](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/gondola-hotel-price-alerts/))

5. **Seats.aero Adds JetBlue Move to Mint Finder + 25+ Programs** — **Neutral.** Seats.aero expanded to 25+ loyalty programs and launched a specialized JetBlue "Move to Mint" upgrade finder, plus Fare Class Viewer and Seat Map Viewer for Pro subscribers. The award search category continues to deepen and professionalize. Seats.aero's $9.99/mo Pro tier is anchoring category pricing. This is all in the award-search lane, not Wayloft's card-decision lane. No action needed. Continued confirmation that Wayloft should stay out of award search — it's crowded (PointsYeah, AwardFares, Seats.aero, Point.me) and well-served. ([Source](https://awardfares.com/blog/awardfares-vs-seats-aero/))

## User Pain Points

- **CSR sticker shock is real and unresolved.** The $795 fee increase left many cardholders genuinely unsure whether to keep, downgrade, or call for retention. Content answers proliferate, but an interactive tool that personalizes the verdict to the user's actual benefit usage (Wayloft's worth-it tool format) is not being served by major content sites. The opportunity is in the tool layer, not another article.
- **Hotel + card points stacking is confusing.** Gondola's growth and feature additions signal users want help understanding the triple-dip (hotel loyalty + card points + portal cashback). No tool walks a user through their full stacking options for a given hotel stay in plain language.
- **Award search fragmentation remains.** Seats.aero expanding to 25+ programs and PointsYeah growing to 22 airline programs shows demand, but users still have to know which tool to use for which program. No single view connects "my card has UR points" → "here's award availability on my target route" — the bridge between card-decision and award-search is still wide open.
- **CardPointers complexity is growing.** As CardPointers adds Apple Intelligence, Vision Pro, ChatGPT, and Mac support, it's moving upmarket toward power users. The 22-35 casual user who just wants "should I keep my Sapphire or downgrade?" is increasingly underserved by CardPointers' expanding complexity. Wayloft's simplicity angle is more defensible than it was a month ago.

## Watch List

- **HeyMax (Singapore/HK)** — Listed on PhocusWire's Hot 25 Travel Startups 2026. Travel-fintech with proprietary Max Miles currency, 1:1 transfers to 30+ partners with no fees. Not yet in the US market. If it launches in the US, it's a potential 6th transfer currency to track and a content angle. Flag for 30-day watch.
- **Thrifty Traveler benefits tracker** — Now covers 50+ cards in a free Google Sheets format. Has TPG coverage and a dedicated domain (cardtracker.thriftytraveler.com). SEO collision with Wayloft's statement credit tracker content is ongoing. Their Google Sheets format is a UX moat Wayloft beats, but they have SEO authority and backlinks Wayloft doesn't yet.
- **CSR worth-it content window** — The window for capturing "CSR worth $795" search traffic narrows every week that Wayloft delays indexing the CSR SSG page. Motley Fool, NerdWallet, and AFAR are already ranking. The window is now, not next month.
- **CardPointers pricing** — The 50% Big Spring Sale ($45/yr) may still be live. If CardPointers normalizes at this price point, it signals the category ceiling for entry-level card optimization is around $45-50/yr. Wayloft's $9.99/mo ($79/yr annual) Pro tier is above this. Worth watching whether the sale ends or becomes permanent.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Ship the CSR worth-it SSG page this week.** The $795 fee increase is the biggest card repricing story of 2026 and the search intent is live right now. Wayloft's `computeValueBreakdown()` already handles it. Target: "Chase Sapphire Reserve worth the annual fee 2026," "is the CSR worth $795," "Chase Sapphire Reserve keep or downgrade." Ellis Church byline, shareable OG card. This is Layer 1 distribution — don't wait.

2. **Publish a positioning piece: "Ellis Church vs. ChatGPT wrappers."** CardPointers v7 now has ChatGPT + Siri integration. Write content (or an Ellis Church post) distinguishing native, opinionated analysis from bolting a generic AI onto card data. The angle: a ChatGPT wrapper doesn't know you — Ellis Church has a point of view. This defends the AI persona angle before cardPointers builds more mindshare around it.

3. **Add Gondola as a recommended tool in hotel spend content.** Gondola is not a competitor. Mentioning it in Wayloft content ("use Gondola for hotel booking, Wayloft to decide if your card is worth keeping") signals expertise, drives topical authority, and may generate a reciprocal mention. Easy content win with no build cost.

4. **Stay out of award search.** PointsYeah, Seats.aero, AwardFares, and Point.me are all actively investing here. NerdWallet is driving traffic to PointsYeah. The category is well-served. Wayloft's moat is card-decision — don't dilute it.

5. **Watch HeyMax for US launch.** If it enters the US market with its transfer currency model, it becomes a 6th entry for Wayloft's transfer partner database and a potential content angle (Max Miles vs. UR/MR/TYP). No action now; set a 30-day flag.
