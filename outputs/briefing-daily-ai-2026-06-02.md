# Morning Briefing — 2026-06-02

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Mina Meeting Assistant** — This was Product Hunt's top launch of the week, pulling 29,547 votes and landing at #1 for June 1. Unlike passive notetakers (Otter, Fireflies), Mina actually *participates* in your meetings — you say "Hey Mina," it responds live, pulls context from connected tools (Slack, Salesforce, Jira), and can file tickets, update CRMs, and draft follow-ups before the call ends. It integrates with 200+ tools. Worth watching if you're thinking about meeting workflows at Okta. ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/leaderboard/daily/2026/6/1))

2. **OpenClaw crossing 250K GitHub stars** — This open-source agent, launched late 2025, hit 250,000 GitHub stars in under four months — one of the fastest-growing repos on the platform. It runs as a persistent agent on your computer 24/7, not a chatbot you invoke. The Reddit/HN buzz is around its ability to autonomously manage tasks across apps without cloud dependency. It's the clearest signal yet that always-on, local-first AI agents are where builders are headed next. ([AI Tool Discovery](https://www.aitooldiscovery.com/guides/best-ai-tools-reddit))

3. **Local AI going mainstream** — r/LocalLLaMA and related communities are buzzing about Ollama + Gemini 4 Open (Google's 2B/4B/26B/31B open model family, released April 2026 with strong science reasoning and document understanding). The shift: running your own LLM is no longer a hobbyist project — it's becoming the default for privacy-sensitive workflows and cost-sensitive builders. Tools like Jan and GPT4All have filled in the UX gap that used to make this hard. ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/james_miller_8dc58a89cb9e/10-open-source-tools-to-build-production-grade-local-ai-agents-in-2026-say-goodbye-to-sky-high-apis-1ipg))

4. **Gemini 3.5 Pro imminent** — Google launched Flash at I/O (May 19) and Pro is slated for this month. The Flash data already tells the story: it beats Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding and agentic benchmarks, runs 4x faster than competing frontier models, and has a 1M token context window. The tradeoff is pricing tripled vs. the previous Flash. Pro will push further on capability — builders are watching closely to see if Google can sustain the cost/speed advantage into its Pro tier. ([Wavespeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/gemini-3-5-pro-coming-next-month/))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Anthropic files confidential IPO — at a $965B valuation** — Anthropic filed a confidential S-1 with the SEC on June 1, surprising the market by moving before OpenAI. The company just closed a $65B funding round at a $965B post-money valuation — eclipsing OpenAI's $852B for the first time. The revenue growth is staggering: from ~$10B annually a year ago to nearly $47B by May 2026. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley are expected to compete for lead underwriting roles. Target listing date is approximately October 2026, pending SEC review. This is potentially one of the largest IPOs in US history. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-confidentially-files-ipo-965-billion-valuation/) / [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/01/nx-s1-5843199/anthropic-ipo-filing-ai-large))

2. **The frontier race is genuinely neck-and-neck** — Executives at Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all publicly describing the AI frontier race as too close to call — with companies making different bets on cost, speed, and compute rather than any one player pulling away on capability. Google's strategic posture is notable: debuting Gemini 3.5 with the Flash model first (cheap, fast, broadly deployable) rather than a behemoth model signals they're competing for scale and embedded usage rather than benchmark supremacy alone. The race is now as much about distribution and cost as it is about raw model performance. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/21/google-ai-anthropic-openai-war))

3. **AI's impact on early-career workers is getting hard to ignore** — The emerging consensus in tech circles: AI hasn't eliminated jobs wholesale, but it's dramatically compressed the value of entry-level output. The 20% employment decline among 22–25-year-olds in software (noted in yesterday's briefing from Goldman) is now landing in op-eds and think pieces as a structural issue, not a blip. The debate is shifting from "will AI take jobs?" to "what does a career look like when your early years — the learning years — are the ones AI does first?" Relevant for you specifically as you're entering Okta right as this pressure is most acute for new grads. ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news))

## World News

1. **Iran suspends US ceasefire talks — Trump says talks are ongoing** — Iran broke off negotiations with the US on June 1, citing Israeli military escalation in Lebanon as a ceasefire violation. Iranian state media confirmed the suspension; a senior Iranian military officer said a resumption of hostilities with the US was "inevitable." Trump publicly contradicted Iran's account, saying talks are "continuing at a rapid pace." Israel's PM Netanyahu separately stated Israeli forces would "continue to operate as planned in southern Lebanon," complicating any settlement. The situation is highly volatile with no confirmed path to resumption. ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/06/01/iran-us-trade-strikes-deal-end-war-remains-elusive/) / [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-trump-strikes-ceasefire-lebanon-israel/))

2. **Ebola outbreak in Congo nears 300 confirmed cases — Kenya quarantine facility in court today** — The 2026 Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC has hit ~300 confirmed cases and over 1,000 suspected cases; WHO declared a public health emergency in mid-May. A parallel legal dispute is unfolding in Kenya, where a court temporarily blocked a US military plan to establish an Ebola quarantine facility at a Kenyan air base — locals protested in Nanyuki, and the case returns to court today (June 2). The Red Cross has flagged 10 African countries at elevated risk. No approved treatment or vaccine exists for the Bundibugyo strain. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/1/kenyans-protest-planned-us-ebola-quarantine-facility) / [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/data-graphics/ebola-outbreak-2026-cases-virus-tracking-maps-spread-congo-ugangda-us-rcna347102))

3. **Pancreatic cancer drug nearly doubles survival rates — "grand slam" at ASCO 2026** — Daraxonrasib, a RAS inhibitor from Revolution Medicines, delivered Phase 3 results at ASCO 2026 that drew a standing ovation: patients on the drug had median overall survival of 13.2 months vs. 6.6 months on standard chemotherapy — nearly doubling survival. The drug targets a mutated protein (KRAS) present in over 90% of pancreatic cancers, a target that had resisted treatment for decades. It also produced fewer serious side effects than chemo. FDA submission is forthcoming; approval could reach patients within 1-2 years. ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/june-2026/daraxonrasib-nearly-doubles-survival-in-previously-treated-metastatic-pancreatic-cancer/) / [STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/31/pancreatic-cancer-daraxonrasib-revolution-medicines-results-asco-2026/))

4. **California gubernatorial primary today** — California holds its primary today to choose who will succeed Gavin Newsom (term-limited). The race will advance the top two finishers to a general election, regardless of party. A competitive field of Democrats and Republicans is vying for position in a race that will shape the country's largest state for years. Results expected tonight. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/6/1/who-will-replace-newsom-what-to-know-about-californias-primary-elections))
