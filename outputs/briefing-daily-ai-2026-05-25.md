# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-25

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Google AI Search — the product actually changed** — Google's I/O announcement has fully landed: Search is no longer a list of links. The redesigned product generates custom visuals, interactive graphics, and mini-apps inline in response to queries, pulling and synthesizing sources from across the web in real time. Google calls it "the biggest change in more than 25 years." Publishers, SEO professionals, and anyone building traffic-dependent businesses are scrambling to figure out what link-based discovery even means now. For Wayloft specifically: travel search just got restructured at the query layer — the interface sitting between users and your content has fundamentally changed. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/23/tech/ai-internet-search) · [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/20/google-search-ai-internet/))

2. **Contextberg — live context for AI coding agents** — Getting traction this week among Claude Code and Cursor power users: Contextberg is an MCP-compatible tool that continuously feeds your local screen, open browser tabs, and active chat context into your coding agent. It attacks the "#1 failure mode" of AI coding tools — the agent not knowing what you're currently looking at or working on. The Product Hunt AI coding agents category highlights it alongside Google Antigravity and Emdash as part of a new wave of tools focused on context rather than raw code generation. Early users report dramatically fewer "the agent suggested the wrong file" failures in multi-repo workflows. ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/categories/ai-coding-agents))

3. **Claude Code: 4% of GitHub public commits, on pace for 20% by year-end** — Dylan Patel at SemiAnalysis reported that Claude Code now accounts for 4% of all public GitHub commits — a figure that has *doubled in eight weeks*, and which he projects will exceed 20% of all daily commits by the end of 2026. Dario Amodei added the human-scale stat at the Code with Claude conference this week: the average developer using Claude Code spends 20 hours per week with it. That's half a working week. At this point Claude Code isn't a productivity tool — it's restructuring how software development actually happens, which is a big part of why Anthropic flipped the enterprise adoption number (see Industry section). ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/claude-code-is-the-inflection-point) · [Dylan Patel on X](https://x.com/dylan522p/status/2019490550911766763))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Pope Leo XIV publishes *Magnifica Humanitas* — the world's first papal encyclical on AI** — Today, May 25, Pope Leo XIV released his first encyclical, titled *Magnifica Humanitas* ("Magnificent Humanity"), devoted entirely to protecting human dignity in the age of artificial intelligence. The 245-paragraph document was signed on May 15 — the 135th anniversary of Pope Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum*, the encyclical that established Catholic social teaching on labor rights and economic justice. Anthropic co-founder Christopher Olah is among the speakers presenting the text at the Vatican's Synod Hall. The significance: AI has now officially entered the Catholic social doctrine canon, joining labor, property, and economic equality as matters of formal Church teaching — reaching 1.4 billion people. This is the most consequential cultural legitimacy event AI has received outside the tech world. ([Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-05/pope-leo-xiv-first-encyclical-magnifica-humanitas.html) · [America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/vatican-dispatch/2026/05/18/pope-leo-encyclical-artifical-intelligence-anthropic/))

2. **Anthropic overtakes OpenAI in U.S. business AI payments — first time ever** — The May 2026 Ramp AI Index (tracking actual corporate card spend across tens of thousands of US businesses) shows Anthropic at 34.4% of businesses paying for AI, OpenAI at 32.3%. This is the first time in the AI industry's history that Anthropic has held the top position by paid business subscriptions. The engine is Claude Code: enterprise $1M+ accounts doubled from 500 to 1,000 between February and May. OpenAI peaked around 36.5% in mid-2025 and has been gradually ceding ground. VentureBeat flags three threats to Anthropic's lead: escalating compute costs ($1.25B/month to Google Cloud), the fragility of token-based pricing as OpenAI pursues flat-rate agent plans, and OpenAI's own enterprise verticalization push. The lead is real but not structurally secure. ([Ramp AI Index](https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-index-may-2026) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-finally-beat-openai-in-business-ai-adoption-but-3-big-threats-could-erase-its-lead))

3. **The world is realizing AI didn't upgrade search — it replaced it** — Google's AI search overhaul is generating a second wave of coverage that's less about features and more about consequences. The question being widely asked: when Search generates a custom interactive app in response to every query, what happens to the rest of the web? CNN ran a major piece titled "AI is changing the internet forever." SEO-dependent businesses (media, e-commerce, content marketing) are facing an extinction-level rethinking of traffic acquisition. Axios is reporting that publishers are already negotiating directly with Google over content licensing terms, a dynamic that would have been unthinkable three years ago. The realization hitting this week: this isn't a search upgrade, it's a platform shift — and the beneficiary isn't Google search users or Google search results, it's Google. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/23/tech/ai-internet-search) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/14/anthropic-claude-price-openai-tokens))

4. **Gemini 3.5 Pro delayed to June — crowd at I/O groaned** — A detail from the Google I/O 2026 presentation that didn't make most headlines but is circulating among practitioners: Sundar Pichai announced that Gemini 3.5 Pro won't ship until June, a beat after the Flash launch. The live audience audibly groaned. Flash launched with strong benchmarks (76.2% Terminal-Bench, 4× speed over previous generation, $1.50/$9 per million tokens) but practitioners had expected Pro to be the headline model. The delay has spawned discussion on X about whether Google's frontier model pipeline is stretched — with DeepMind reportedly under pressure to deliver differentiated research results while the product side ships consumer releases. Worth watching when Pro actually drops in June. ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/05/20/google-introduces-gemini-3-5-flash-at-i-o-2026-a-faster-and-cheaper-model-for-ai-agents-and-coding/) · [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/google-delays-gemini-35-pro-releases-35-flash-at-io-0dcab6cd))

## World News

1. **Russia deploys nuclear-capable hypersonic missile in largest-ever attack on Kyiv** — Overnight May 23–24, Russia launched what Ukrainian officials described as the largest single attack on Kyiv since the full-scale war began: 600 drones and 90 missiles, including a confirmed deployment of the Oreshnik medium-range ballistic missile — its third use in the war and the first at this scale. The Oreshnik travels at hypersonic speed and is designed to defeat air defense systems; Ukraine cannot reliably intercept it. The museum dedicated to the 1986 Chernobyl nuclear disaster was destroyed. Four people were killed, over 80 wounded, and dozens of residential buildings and several schools were damaged. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy confirmed the Oreshnik struck Bila Tserkva, a city of 200,000 people 40 miles from Kyiv's outskirts. Russia's Defense Ministry framed the attack as "a response to Ukraine's terrorist attacks on civilian targets within Russian territory." ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/24/russia-hits-ukraine-with-hypersonic-missile-in-massive-attack-on-kyiv.html) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/24/nx-s1-5833050/russia-uses-hypersonic-oreshnik-missile-in-mass-attack-on-kyiv) · [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-attack-may-24-2026/))

2. **WHO declares Ebola a global health emergency — no vaccine for this strain** — The World Health Organization declared the DRC-Uganda Ebola outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on May 17 — the highest alarm level under international health law. As of May 23: 968 suspected cases and at least 216 deaths, concentrated in DRC's Ituri, Nord-Kivu, and Sud-Kivu provinces, with five confirmed cases now in Kampala, Uganda's capital. The strain is Bundibugyo virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment. A US Embassy advisory for Uganda is active. The CDC rates the risk to the United States as low but has issued a health advisory. The WHO's emergency committee issued temporary recommendations for border screening and international coordination; the outbreak is the 17th recorded Ebola event in DRC since 1976. ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) · [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/han/php/notices/han00530.html) · [ECDC](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-virus-disease-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-and-uganda-19-may-2026))

3. **China launches Shenzhou 23 — one astronaut to stay in space a full year** — China sent three astronauts to its Tiangong space station aboard Shenzhou 23. One crew member is scheduled to remain aboard for an extended 12-month mission — a duration designed to generate data on human physiological adaptation to long-duration spaceflight, directly supporting China's lunar and deep-space ambitions. The mission puts China on a trajectory comparable to the ISS annual mission program, which NASA has used since 2015. China's crewed spaceflight program has accelerated significantly in the past 18 months, and back-to-back extended missions signal a maturing operational tempo rather than milestone launches.
