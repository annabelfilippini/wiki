# Morning Briefing — 2026-04-28

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Cursor 3: You're the Architect Now** — Cursor's biggest release since forking VS Code shipped April 2 and the ecosystem is still chewing on it. The headline feature is the Agents Window — a full-screen workspace for running multiple AI agents in parallel across repos and tasks simultaneously — alongside Design Mode for visually directing UI changes and Composer 2 (61.3 SWE-bench), Cursor's own in-house model. Developer reaction is split: the top Hacker News comment on launch day was a plea to keep the old philosophy of developer-drives-agent; a Cursor engineer fired back that the classic IDE is still there and the Agents Window is optional. The real debate isn't about features — it's about whether you want to be an architect or a coder going forward. ([Cursor Blog](https://cursor.com/blog/cursor-3), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/04/cursor-3-agent-first-interface/))

2. **DeepSeek V4: Frontier Performance, Consumer Pricing** — DeepSeek dropped V4-Pro and V4-Flash on April 24, and the benchmarks are hard to ignore. V4-Pro (1.6T total / 49B active params) matches GPT-5.4 on several coding benchmarks — outperforming Claude on LiveCodeBench (93.5% vs 88.8%) and topping Codeforces ratings — at $0.145/M input tokens. V4-Flash is even cheaper at $0.14/M. Both are open weights. Knowledge gaps vs. top closed-source models are real but narrowing; the price-performance ratio is the story. This keeps the pressure on Western labs to justify frontier pricing. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/deepseek-previews-new-ai-model-that-closes-the-gap-with-frontier-models/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/24/deepseek_v4/))

3. **The App-Building Boom Is Real** — App Store releases are up 104% year-over-year in April 2026, and AI tools are the direct cause. Lovable hit $20M ARR within two months of launch — fastest for any AI app builder in history. The pattern: Cursor, Claude Code, GitHub Copilot enterprise have collapsed the cost of shipping software, and an army of solo builders is walking through that door. Worth flagging for Wayloft specifically — the "we can build this" moat is compressing fast as non-engineers ship production apps. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/))

4. **Claude Code Now Powers GitHub Copilot Enterprise** — Claude Code, the terminal-based coding agent, is now the engine behind GitHub Copilot's enterprise tier. It reads full codebases, plans multi-file changes, runs tests, and supports multi-agent coordination for parallelizing large tasks. This is Claude Code's first major enterprise distribution play — and it puts it directly in the workflow of developers who never would have sought out a separate CLI tool. For builders watching the IDE wars, Cursor vs. Copilot-enterprise is now the main match. ([DataNorth AI](https://datanorth.ai/blog/top-10-ai-tools-for-2026))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Google Bets $40B on Anthropic — Its Own Competitor** — On April 24, Google committed up to $40B in Anthropic: $10B now at a $350B valuation, with another $30B contingent on performance milestones. Amazon already has a $5B+$20B deal in place. Anthropic's annualized revenue has crossed $30B. The dynamic is strange: Anthropic is simultaneously a Google DeepMind rival, a GCP cloud customer, and now a Google portfolio company. Cloud providers are deciding it's better to fund the competition than lose the infrastructure revenue — and nobody wants to be the one who didn't back the winner. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-24/google-plans-to-invest-up-to-40-billion-in-anthropic))

2. **Meta Cuts 8,000 Jobs to Fund $135B AI Push** — Meta announced April 23 it's laying off 10% of its workforce (~8,000 people, effective May 20) while leaving 6,000 open roles unfilled. The company spent $72B on AI capex in 2025 and is heading to $115B+ in 2026. Combined with Microsoft's simultaneous 6,000-person cut, two companies shed 20,000 jobs in a single week. Zuckerberg called 2026 "the year AI dramatically changes how we work" — this is what that looks like from the inside. The trade is explicit: headcount for compute. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/23/tech/meta-layoffs-10-percent-staff-ai), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/24/20k-job-cuts-at-meta-microsoft-raise-concern-of-ai-labor-crisis-.html))

3. **Top AI Talent Is Fleeing Big Tech for Their Own Startups** — Today's CNBC report documents the accelerating exodus: senior researchers from Meta, Google, and OpenAI are leaving to found companies, and VCs are writing $300M checks before the business plan exists (Periodic Labs, ex-OpenAI and DeepMind staff, raised $300M post-launch). $18.8B has flowed to AI startups founded since 2025. The pull is research freedom — the big labs are commercial-first now, leaving little room for exploratory work outside the dominant LLM paradigm. The irony: companies investing the most in AI are losing their best AI people. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/28/meta-google-big-tech-staff-ai-labs-investors.html))

4. **OpenAI Crosses $25B Revenue, Eyes Late-2026 IPO** — OpenAI has surpassed $25B in annualized revenue and is reportedly taking early steps toward a public listing as soon as late 2026. Anthropic is approaching $30B. The internal tension between Altman (wants Q4 IPO) and the CFO (citing a $14B projected loss) hasn't resolved publicly, but the revenue trajectory makes the "loss-leader to dominance" pitch easier to take to public markets. If it happens, it'll be one of the largest tech IPOs ever filed. ([LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/08/meta-debuts-first-major-ai-model-since-14-billion-deal-to-bring-in-alexandr-wang.html))

## World News

1. **Iran Peace Talks Stall — Hormuz Blockade Continues (Day 59)** — Iran submitted a proposal to end the war: reopen the Strait of Hormuz, halt fighting, and defer nuclear negotiations for later. Trump's national security team is reviewing it, but multiple sources tell CNN and NBC that Trump is not currently open to the offer. Iran's Foreign Minister met Putin in St. Petersburg seeking diplomatic backing as US-mediated talks remain on hold. The UN Secretary-General warned the blockade risks triggering a global food emergency. Washington's core hesitation: reopening the strait without nuclear concessions eliminates key US leverage. Sources say the two sides are not as far apart as they appear. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/4/28/iran-war-live-trump-reviews-peace-plan-un-calls-for-hormuz-to-reopen), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/27/world/live-news/iran-war-trump-israel))

2. **King Charles in Washington for First Royal State Visit Since 2007** — King Charles III and Queen Camilla arrived in Washington on April 27 for a four-day state visit (through April 30). Tonight's White House state banquet is the centerpiece event; Charles addresses a joint session of Congress tomorrow. The visit is timed to the US Semiquincentennial — the 250th anniversary of American independence — and is framed around the enduring US-UK "special relationship." The last time a British monarch made a state visit was Queen Elizabeth II in 2007 under President Bush. After Washington, the royals move to New York and Virginia. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/king-charles-visit-trump-us-uk-relations/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/27/us/live-news/king-charles-queen-camilla-us-visit))

3. **US Tariffs Push Recession Odds Above 40%** — Goldman Sachs now puts US recession probability at 45% over the next 12 months; JP Morgan puts the global recession risk at 60%. Consumer confidence fell in April to its lowest level since COVID. The current effective US tariff rate is 11% — the highest since 1943. Yale Budget Lab estimates that if permanent tariffs hold, average households lose $1,200-$1,500 annually. The IMF flagged stagflation — simultaneous inflation and unemployment — as its primary downside risk. Tariff policy continues to evolve; the Yale Budget Lab logged another round of adjustments in its April 8 update. ([Yale Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-april-8-2026), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/07/nx-s1-5354927/recession-trump-tariffs))

4. **IMF Cuts Global Growth Forecast, Cites War and Tariffs** — The IMF's April 2026 World Economic Outlook — titled "Global Economy in the Shadow of War" — cut global growth projections from 3.4% to 3.1% for 2026. Global inflation expected to rise to 4.4%. The two named drivers: the Iran war and US tariff policy. In the IMF's adverse scenario, growth falls to 2.5% with inflation at 5.4%; in a severe scenario, growth drops to 2% and inflation exceeds 6%. Emerging market and commodity-importing economies face the sharpest risks. ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/04/14/world-economic-outlook-april-2026))
