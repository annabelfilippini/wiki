# Wayloft Competitor Brief — 2026-05-18

## Top 5

1. **CardPointers: MCP AI integration launched March 27** — **Threat.** Wayloft's closest competitor shipped a Model Context Protocol connector that plugs real cardholder data (balances, credits, expiring offers, annual fees) directly into ChatGPT, Claude, and any MCP-compatible AI assistant. CardPointers+ subscribers ($90/yr) can now ask natural-language questions like "what should I do with my Amex Gold this month?" and get answers grounded in their actual card data. This is a direct attack on Ellis Church's differentiation — they've productized the same "AI that knows your cards" promise. **What to watch:** Their approach has a structural weakness: it requires CardPointers+ AND a ChatGPT/Claude subscription. That's two paywalls, two accounts, zero integrated UX. Wayloft's opportunity is to make Ellis Church feel native — built-in, opinionated, zero setup, no external AI required. Don't race them on MCP; race them on experience. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/27/cardpointers-app-can-now-integrate-with-chatgpt-and-more-save-50/))

2. **PointsYeah named NerdWallet's #1 award search tool for 2026** — **Opportunity.** NerdWallet dropped Seats.aero and Point.me from the top spot and replaced them with PointsYeah, a newer entrant offering both flight and hotel award search with a free tier and $11.99/mo premium. Its "Daydream Explorer" feature does inspiration-based searching — you don't need a destination to start. This crowns a new editorial favorite in award search but doesn't touch Wayloft's lane. Wayloft doesn't do award search; it does card portfolio optimization. **The opportunity:** as the award search category gets noisier, Wayloft's worth-it tool is cleaner to find via SEO ("is Amex Gold worth the annual fee") vs. "award search tool" queries that now have five credible options. Leaning into the distinction in content could drive qualified traffic that award search tools don't capture. ([Source](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/best-award-travel-search-tool))

3. **Capital One launched a dedicated travel app on March 17, bringing Hopper tech in-house** — **Neutral, watch.** Capital One ended its Hopper partnership and shipped a native travel app (iOS + Android) that shows miles balance during checkout, surfaces real-time gate changes, and shows lounge wait times. It's issuer-specific — only Capital One cardholders benefit, and only within the Capital One ecosystem. Wayloft is inherently multi-issuer, which remains the right positioning as each major issuer builds its own walled garden. The broader signal: issuers are investing heavily in the UX layer around points. The more fragmented the issuer apps become, the more valuable a neutral, multi-issuer optimizer is. This is a slow tailwind for Wayloft, not a near-term threat. ([Source](https://thepointsguy.com/news/capital-one-new-travel-app/))

4. **Gondola: free hotel points optimizer, $3M seed, gaining traction** — **Neutral/Opportunity.** Gondola launched a free app that connects to a user's bank accounts (not email), tracks Amex MR, Chase UR, and Citi TYP balances automatically, then uses those points for hotel bookings with 16% average cashback. It's entirely free — no subscription, revenue from booking commissions. This is not direct competition with Wayloft's card decision tooling, but it raises the free-tier expectation for the category. Users getting automatic balance tracking for free from Gondola will expect the same from Wayloft. **Wayloft's differentiation:** Gondola optimizes *where you spend points* (hotel redemptions); Wayloft optimizes *which cards to hold and why*. Different questions, complementary tools. A partnership or wikilink cross-referencing could position Wayloft as the upstream decision layer. ([Source](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/gondola-review/))

5. **Rove Miles (YC-backed) added its 18th transfer partner May 7 with 25% bonus** — **Watch.** Rove is a universal loyalty program that works on top of existing credit cards — users earn "Rove Miles" through a shopping/travel portal, transferable to 18 airline and hotel partners. Added a new partner May 7, 2026 with 25% transfer bonus through June 6. Not a direct Wayloft competitor (it's a loyalty program, not a portfolio optimizer), but it's a new transfer currency that Wayloft's transfer bonus tracker doesn't yet cover. If Rove grows to the scale of Bilt (which started similarly), it becomes a sixth transfer currency Wayloft needs to track. Watch for when Rove Miles appears in mainstream Reddit discussion as a signal. ([Source](https://viewfromthewing.com/vc-backed-rove-debuts-universal-airline-mile-instantly-earn-up-to-25x-transfer-to-12-programs-book-140-airlines/))

## User Pain Points

- **Annual fee fatigue is real and growing.** Reddit users are actively posting "is Amex Platinum still worth it after the refresh?" with no good tool to calculator the answer for their specific usage. The keep/call/downgrade verdict in Wayloft's worth-it tool is exactly what these posts are asking for — it just needs to be findable.
- **CardPointers' AI double-paywall frustration.** The MCP integration requires CardPointers+ ($90/yr) *plus* a ChatGPT Plus subscription. Forum users are noting the cost adds up. "I pay $90 for CardPointers and $20/mo for ChatGPT just to get a recommendation I should be getting natively."
- **Group award travel is an unsolved mess.** Recurring r/awardtravel complaint: no tool handles multi-person award bookings well. Award availability for 2+ passengers on the same itinerary is a manual nightmare. Long-term product signal for Wayloft's flight search feature (currently blocked by business registration).
- **92% of miles go unredeemed.** The education gap remains massive. Users who could benefit from Wayloft's tools don't know what to search for — they don't know what they're missing. Content framing around "you probably have $500 sitting in points" could reach this audience.
- **Phantom award availability.** PointsYeah's biggest user complaint: showing award space that isn't actually bookable. No competitor has solved this cleanly. Not a Wayloft problem today (Wayloft doesn't do award search), but worth noting if flight search activates.

## Watch List

- **AI-in-the-loop card optimization is the emerging standard.** CardPointers v7 MCP is the first major implementation; expect MaxRewards to announce something similar within 60-90 days. Wayloft should ship Ellis Church's first public-facing output (worth-it analysis pages) before this becomes table stakes.
- **Issuers building native apps.** Capital One this week, Amex and Chase have similar products in various states. Each issuer app is a walled garden — good for Wayloft's multi-issuer positioning, but potential long-term pressure if issuers lock data.
- **Seats.aero pricing pressure.** AwardFares launched revamped Gold ($9.99/mo) and Diamond ($19.99/mo) tiers in direct competition with Seats.aero's $9.99/mo Pro. The award search tools are in a pricing war. Not Wayloft's fight, but watch for valuation signals or acquisition activity.
- **PointsYeah hotel + flight integration.** The move to combine hotel AND flight award search in one tool is significant UX consolidation. If a competitor extends this pattern into card portfolio management (one tool for search + optimization), Wayloft's scope advantage narrows.

---

## Recommended Actions

1. **Launch worth-it tool SSG pages now, before "annual fee decision" becomes contested SEO territory.** CardPointers' annual fee tracking is locked behind $90/yr and is not indexed by Google. Wayloft's public SSG pages ("/credit-cards/amex-gold/worth-it") are a durable SEO moat that no current competitor has. Every week without those pages live is forgone organic ranking.

2. **Reposition Ellis Church explicitly against CardPointers' MCP approach.** The differentiating message writes itself: "CardPointers sends your data to ChatGPT and you pay twice. Ellis Church is built in." Draft a landing page section or content piece that names this gap without naming the competitor. Frame it as "native AI vs. AI plumbing."

3. **Add Rove Miles to the transfer bonus tracker's roadmap.** It's the sixth transferable currency with real traction, a YC pedigree, and a 25% transfer bonus active now through June 6. Adding Rove makes Wayloft more complete than any competitor's database. Low effort, defensible data moat.

4. **Don't build award search.** PointsYeah, Seats.aero, AwardFares, and Point.me are all well-funded and excellent at award search. Wayloft's moat is the upstream decision: "should I even have this card?" Award search is what you do *after* you've made the card decision Wayloft helps with. Keeping the scope clean makes Wayloft easier to explain and rank for.

5. **Free tier must stay meaningfully free.** Gondola ($0, funded by booking commissions) is setting the expectation that core points tracking is free. Wayloft's free tier needs to deliver real value — the worth-it tool, card recommendations, and at least basic portfolio tracking — to compete with zero-dollar tools for early user acquisition.
