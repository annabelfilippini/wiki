# Wayloft Competitor Brief — 2026-04-13

## Top 5

1. **CardPointers: v7 launches with MCP + AI integration — Wayloft's closest competitor just got smarter** — **Threat.** CardPointers shipped v7 in late March 2026 with a full redesign and a headline feature: MCP integration that lets CardPointers+ users query their card data directly through ChatGPT, Claude, Siri/Apple Intelligence, or any MCP-compatible AI. Ask "which card should I use at Whole Foods this week?" and the LLM answers with real card data. They ran a Big Spring Sale (50% off through April 9 — lifetime access at $140 one-time). CardPointers is Wayloft's most direct competitor in portfolio optimization. They've now added AI-native access to card data, which is the direction the whole space is going. Ellis Church as a persona is differentiated, but Wayloft needs a clear answer to "why not just plug CardPointers into Claude?" The MCP angle is also a build signal: exposing Wayloft's Worth-It logic and transfer bonus data as an MCP server could be a fast, high-leverage move. ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/27/cardpointers-app-can-now-integrate-with-chatgpt-and-more-save-50/), [CardPointers](https://cardpointers.com/ai/))

2. **Point.me Explore: "Google Flights for points" feature, 30% faster searches** — **Opportunity.** Point.me rolled out an "Explore" feature that lets you pick a home airport and browse award destinations by date — the same interaction model as Google Flights Explore, but for points. Search speeds improved ~30% (initial results in ~7 seconds). They also added co-terminal search and easier multi-date checking. Point.me's niche is award flight search — not Wayloft's territory today. But this is the gold standard UX direction the space is moving toward. More importantly: Point.me is pulling users who want flexible award discovery, which is a Layer 2 audience for Wayloft (users who already have points and want to use them). A "what can I do with my Chase points?" integration or editorial content series could capture this intent. ([AwardWallet Blog](https://awardwallet.com/travel/point-me-explore-tool/?pubDate=2026-04-10), [Thrifty Traveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/news/points/pointme-explore/))

3. **AwardWallet pivots to content with "AwardWallet Learn" — balance tracker becomes media company** — **Neutral, watch.** AwardWallet launched a dedicated editorial site called AwardWallet Learn on Feb 24, publishing close to 2,500 posts in 2025. The new site is a clean break from the balance-tracker product — it's now competing directly for SEO on points/miles content. AwardWallet's co-founder also co-founded Point.me, so they're quietly assembling a vertical stack: track balances → find award flights → editorial coverage. They're not yet in annual-fee decision or portfolio optimization territory. But the content play means the SEO competition is heating up fast. Wayloft's Worth-It SEO moat ("[card] worth it annual fee" queries) needs to be staked out now, before AwardWallet's content machine gets there. ([AwardWallet Learn](https://awardwallet.com/learn/))

4. **TPG app has "cash or points" flight verdict tool — not the same as Worth-It, but same instinct** — **Neutral.** The Points Guy app includes a tool that tells users whether to pay cash or use points for a specific flight booking. It's redemption-focused (flight bookings), not annual-fee-focused — so it doesn't directly overlap with Wayloft's Worth-It tool. But it signals that the "give me a verdict, not a spreadsheet" UX pattern has mainstream validation. TPG has 800K+ app users and significant brand trust. The differentiation Wayloft needs to maintain: TPG tells you what to do with a specific redemption; Wayloft tells you whether your portfolio makes sense at all. That's a more durable relationship. ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/travel/tpg-trends-flexibility-maximizing-tools/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/select/the-points-guy-launches-app-to-help-track-your-credit-card-rewards/))

5. **Rove Miles hits 17 transfer partners, targets Gen Z with no-credit-card earn model** — **Opportunity.** Rove Miles (Y Combinator-backed, ~$2M seed, founded by a 22-year-old) now has 17 airline/hotel transfer partners after adding JAL with a 50% launch bonus and Virgin Atlantic/Virgin Red. The pitch: earn miles by shopping and staying, without a credit card — stack on top of whatever card you already have. It's explicitly Gen Z-targeted and gaining media traction (TechCrunch, TPG, Upgraded Points). Rove is building a new point currency from scratch, which means it's undercovered and untrusted by most tools. Wayloft has 5 transfer currencies tracked; adding Rove Miles as a sixth — with "what are Rove Miles actually worth?" as the SEO wedge — could be a fast content win and a genuine data asset before competitors notice. The audience overlap is high: Rove's users are exactly Wayloft's target demographic. ([Rove](https://www.rove.com/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/07/rove-founded-by-a-22-year-old-is-helping-gen-z-earn-airline-miles-without-credit-cards/), [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/travel/rove-miles-rewards-program-review/))

## User Pain Points

- **Award search tool reliability:** Users frustrated by phantom availability, incorrect Delta SkyMiles pricing, and desktop-only tools (no good mobile experience for award hunting). Not Wayloft's territory today, but signals that polish and mobile-first wins.
- **"Worth it" content is all static editorial:** Every major publisher (Bankrate, Motley Fool, NerdWallet, Experian) has a generic article on "is a credit card annual fee worth it?" but none offer an interactive, per-card, benefit-toggle tool. Wayloft's Worth-It tool is the only one. This gap is real and unaddressed — the SEO competition is weak prose, not product.
- **Card recommendation flow is community-maintained:** The r/Churning flowchart is a hand-maintained Google link. It's widely used (~600K members) but not dynamic. Wayloft's 5-step quiz recommendation engine is already better — it just needs distribution.
- **No tool covers Rove Miles:** Rove is growing fast but almost no existing tools track or analyze it. Users asking "are Rove Miles worth it?" have nowhere to go.
- **r/awardtravel went private:** The primary award travel community is no longer public. Users are scattered and looking for a new home. This is a distribution opportunity — Ellis Church content in r/creditcards and r/churning could fill the gap.

## Watch List

- **MCP as the points optimization interface:** CardPointers' MCP launch is an early signal that credit card data will move into AI assistants. Whoever builds the richest, most trusted MCP server for points data wins the AI-native user. Watch whether Seats.aero, MaxRewards, or TPG follow CardPointers here.
- **Capital One building its own travel app:** Capital One Travel launched a dedicated booking + rewards app. Issuers building direct tools is the long-term threat to the whole third-party ecosystem. 3-5 year horizon, but directionally important.
- **MaxRewards v3 on-demand card recommendations:** MaxRewards v3.102.0 added on-demand personalized card recommendation generation — same category as Wayloft's card recommendation engine. MaxRewards has 800K+ users as distribution leverage.
- **PointsYeah vs. Seats.aero emerging as new award search challenger:** A comparison article framing PointsYeah as a Seats.aero competitor surfaced — worth monitoring for a new entrant in the award search space.
- **Avion Rewards portal rebuild (Canada):** RBC Avion is building a new booking portal with fare tracking and flexible booking tools, launching later in 2026. Not a US threat but signals the institutional push into the optimizer space.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Stake the "Rove Miles worth it" SEO query now.** Rove is at 17 partners, growing, and essentially uncovered by any tool. Write a Worth-It style analysis page for Rove Miles as a transfer currency — CPP valuation, best redemption paths, comparison to UR/MR/C1. This is a first-mover SEO opportunity with a high-intent, emerging audience. Low effort, high upside.

2. **Define Wayloft's MCP answer before CardPointers defines the category.** CardPointers just shipped MCP integration. The question "why not just plug CardPointers into Claude?" will come up. The answer needs to be ready. Options: (a) build a Wayloft MCP server exposing Worth-It logic and transfer bonus data as LLM-queryable tools — Ellis Church as the MCP identity; (b) lean harder into the editorial/persona angle (CardPointers is data, Wayloft is judgment). Either way, have a clear answer.

3. **Publish Worth-It pages for the top 5 cards before AwardWallet's content machine notices the gap.** AwardWallet Learn just stood up 2,500 posts of editorial infrastructure. The annual-fee "worth it" query space is currently under-served by interactive tools. Wayloft has the tool built. Prioritize getting the top 5 cards (CSP, AMEX Gold, Venture X, Platinum, CFU) indexed and ranking.

4. **Draft the Rove Miles entry for the transfer partners database.** Add it as the sixth currency with partner mappings and a CPP valuation estimate. Even a rough valuation is better than nothing and establishes Wayloft as the first tool to cover it seriously.

5. **Stay the course on Worth-It as the Reddit launch vehicle.** The "annual fee" content gap is real and competitively uncontested by interactive tools. CardPointers, TPG, MaxRewards, and AwardWallet are all focused elsewhere. This is Wayloft's clean lane.
