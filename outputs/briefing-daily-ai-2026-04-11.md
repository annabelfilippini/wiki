# Morning Briefing — 2026-04-11

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Claw Code** — An open-source AI coding agent framework built in Python and Rust that launched April 2 and blew past 72,000 GitHub stars within days. Unlike point tools, it's a foundation for building AI *software developers* — agents that can ingest a codebase, plan changes, write code, run tests, and fix their own bugs iteratively. The pace of adoption signals that the dev community has been waiting for a modular, open alternative to the closed agent stacks. Worth watching closely if you want to build Wayloft features with AI-native tooling. ([24-7 Press Release](https://www.24-7pressrelease.com/press-release/533389/claw-code-launches-open-source-ai-coding-agent-framework-with-72000-github-stars-in-first-days))

2. **The 73% Threshold** — New data shows 73% of professional developers now use AI coding tools daily, up from 44% a year ago. The more important shift: the category itself has moved. The 2026 tools aren't autocomplete — they're agents that plan, write, test, and debug end-to-end. Feb–April 2026 is the densest model release window in AI history (7 major releases in February alone), and the gap between "AI assists a human" and "AI does the task" is closing fast. The tools are real; the question is whether workflows have caught up. ([Faros AI](https://www.faros.ai/blog/best-ai-coding-agents-2026), [Robylon](https://www.robylon.ai/blog/leading-ai-coding-agents-of-2026))

3. **Grok Goes Platform-Wide on X** — xAI has opened image and video generation via Grok to all X users on mobile, with Hotshot text-to-video integration coming next. X is also rolling out automatic post translation and an AI-powered photo editor with text-prompt edits. This is less about Grok being the best model and more about X becoming the first major social platform to natively embed multimodal AI generation into the core feed experience — a meaningful UX bet that others will have to respond to. ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-tests-ai-content-labels/812913/))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Big Three vs. China: The Anti-Distillation Coalition** — OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google announced April 6–7 that they are sharing intelligence through the Frontier Model Forum to detect and block adversarial distillation — where outside labs flood U.S. models with automated prompts to clone their behavior without the training cost. Anthropic named three Chinese firms (DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax) that collectively created 24,000 fraudulent accounts and ran 16 million exchanges with Claude to extract capabilities, then stripped safety guardrails from what they copied. This is the first time the three rivals have formally coordinated on a shared threat. The framing — as a national security and IP issue — suggests regulatory action isn't far behind. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/openai-anthropic-google-unite-to-combat-model-copying-in-china), [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/openai-google-anthropic-ai-model-theft-china))

2. **Anthropic's Chip Ambition and $30B Run Rate** — Anthropic's annualized revenue has tripled to $30B since end-2025, and the company is now in exploratory talks about designing custom silicon — joining OpenAI and Meta in the move away from Nvidia dependency. No team, no commitment yet. Simultaneously, Anthropic just signed a long-term deal with Google and Broadcom for ~3.5 gigawatts of TPU compute from 2027, roughly 3x its current consumption. The chip exploration and the Google deal aren't contradictory — they're hedges. Anthropic is growing fast enough to justify both. ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/07/broadcom_google_chip_deal_anthropic_customer/), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-custom-ai-chips-30-billion-revenue))

3. **Treasury and Fed Convene Over AI Cyber Risk** — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Fed Chair Jerome Powell held an unscheduled meeting with major Wall Street executives this week, driven by concerns that Anthropic's latest model release could heighten cyber risks for the financial system. This is notable because it's the first time regulators have treated a single AI model as a potential systemic risk event warranting emergency coordination — not a breach, not a hack, just a new model capability. Financial regulators are clearly operating on the assumption that the gap between AI capability and institutional preparedness is dangerously wide. ([GMA News / AP sourced](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/983455/live-updates-conflict-in-the-middle-east-april-11-2026/story/))

4. **The Jobs Question: Still No Clean Answer** — Fortune's April 1 analysis argues that despite massive AI investment, measurable productivity gains and job displacement remain modest — most companies are spending on AI without clear ROI. But investors are calling 2026 "the year of agents," with AI shifting from productivity tool to actual labor replacement in specific domains. Surveys show employers are already cutting entry-level positions. The honest picture: 11.7% of jobs are technically automatable now, but the organizational and workflow changes needed to capture that aren't happening at speed. The hype and the reality are both true, just on different timelines. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-layoffs-automation-productivity-finance-employment-investors-ceos/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/31/investors-predict-ai-is-coming-for-labor-in-2026/))

## World News

1. **US-Iran Peace Talks Open in Islamabad** — VP JD Vance arrived in Pakistan's capital today to lead ceasefire negotiations — the highest-level US-Iran talks since the 1979 Islamic revolution. The Iranian delegation includes Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. The war, now six weeks old, has killed thousands and destabilized the Middle East. The two sides are far apart: Iran's 10-point proposal demands Hormuz oversight and US troop withdrawal from the region; the US is insisting Iran surrender its enriched uranium stockpile as a non-negotiable. Ceasefire is nominally in effect but fragile. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/4/11/iran-war-live-us-negotiators-due-to-arrive-in-pakistan-for-ceasefire-talks), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-11/vance-arrives-in-pakistan-as-us-iran-peace-talks-set-to-kick-off))

2. **The Strait of Hormuz: Ceasefire, But Still Blocked** — The ceasefire agreed April 8 was supposed to reopen the strait. It hasn't. As of today, only two oil tankers have transited Hormuz since the agreement. Iran is charging over $1 million per ship and requiring explicit passage permission. Pre-war, 100+ vessels moved through daily carrying ~10 million barrels of oil. The 90%+ reduction in traffic is continuing to push up energy and agricultural input costs globally. The Islamabad talks today will need to resolve Hormuz access specifically — it's the core economic pressure point keeping both sides at the table. ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/iran/strait-hormuz-shipping-traffic-effectively-standstill-iran-ceasefire-rcna267391), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/10/shipping-in-strait-of-hormuz-at-a-trickle-despite-us-iran-ceasefire))

3. **Artemis II Crew Splashes Down** — Four astronauts returned to Earth April 10, splashing down in the Pacific near San Diego after a 10-day lunar flyby — the first crewed mission beyond low Earth orbit since Apollo 17 in 1972. Crew: NASA's Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, and Christina Koch, plus CSA's Jeremy Hansen. Orion flew 700,237 miles, peaked at 24,664 mph, and landed within one mile of its target. Recovery teams extracted the crew within two hours. Artemis III (lunar landing) is next. ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/04/10/artemis-ii-flight-day-10-crew-sets-for-final-burn-splashdown/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/artemis-ii-splashdown-return/))

4. **Tariff War: SCOTUS Ruled, But Trade Disruption Continues** — The Supreme Court struck down Trump's IEEPA-based tariffs in February 2026, and lower courts have ordered ~$166 billion in refunds. But the administration is rebuilding the tariff regime under Sections 122, 301, and 232 — and the effective rates remain significantly above pre-Trump levels. The IMF and tax analysts estimate the tariffs still in force represent the largest US tax increase as a percentage of GDP since 1993, averaging $1,500 per household. Meanwhile, global trade is rerouting around the US — other countries are deepening deals with each other rather than waiting for US policy to stabilize. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/03/trump-tariffs-trade-war-impact.html), [Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/), [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/04/01/after-tariffs-global-trade-moves-on-without-us))
