## 1. Iran-US War: Strait of Hormuz Deadline, Ceasefire Rejected

**What happened:** US President Trump has set a deadline of Tuesday (today) for Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, threatening to strike Iranian power plants and bridges if the strait remains closed. Iran rejected the deadline, with a spokesman for Iran's president calling Trump's statement "sheer desperation and anger." Iran says the strait will only reopen "when all the damage caused by the imposed war is compensated through a new legal regime, using a portion of the revenue from transit fees."

**Context:** The US and Israel began the war with surprise airstrikes on Iran on February 28, 2026 (Day 37 as of April 5). Iran responded by closing the Strait of Hormuz — the chokepoint through which roughly 20% of global oil supply passes — a closure described by analysts as the largest disruption to energy supply since the 1970s oil crisis. Only about 5% of pre-war shipping volume is currently getting through. Brent crude has surged sharply above $100/barrel. A 45-day ceasefire proposal is being circulated by international mediators to stave off further escalation.

**Perspectives:**
- The US and Israel frame the war as necessary to prevent Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons and to eliminate state sponsorship of regional militant groups.
- Iran and many non-Western observers frame it as an illegal aggressor war. Al Jazeera notes Iran's ceasefire position centers on war reparations, not unconditional reopening.
- Trump's "Tuesday will be Power Plant Day" statement drew condemnation from European allies and the UN, who warn strikes on civilian infrastructure would constitute war crimes under international humanitarian law.
- Trump says Iran's ceasefire proposal is "significant" but "not good enough." ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/trump-iran-war-ceasefire-hormuz-strait.html))

**Diplomatic thread:** Oman has been brokering talks between Iran and the US. Iran and Oman held fresh discussions on April 5 about "smooth transit" through the strait. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/5/oman-iran-discuss-smooth-transit-in-the-strait-of-hormuz-muscat-says))

## 2. Ukraine: Russia Rejects Easter Ceasefire, Intensifies Attacks

**What happened:** Russia has rejected Ukrainian President Zelensky's proposal for an Orthodox Easter ceasefire (Easter falls April 12). The Kremlin called the proposal a "PR stunt to get a 1-2 month truce to replenish the Ukrainian armed forces." Russia has continued missile and drone attacks on Ukraine, including a mass daytime drone strike. Zelensky described the attacks as "Russia's response to our proposal" and called it an "Easter escalation." He spoke with Pope Leo on Friday.

**Context:** Russian forces occupy roughly 20% of Ukraine as of April 2026. There were 108 combat clashes along the frontline on April 5. Russia gained approximately 17 square miles of Ukrainian territory in the week of March 24–31. Ceasefire negotiations have stalled repeatedly since late 2025. Zelensky is also calling for a mutual halt on strikes against energy infrastructure, citing pressure from rising oil prices due to the Iran war.

**Additional stress:** Ukraine's allies are delivering fewer weapons due to diversion of resources to the Iran theater. Zelensky has publicly acknowledged that Western attention and aid to Ukraine is waning. Bloomberg has reported that Iran's oil windfall is directly financing Russia's continued military campaign.

## 3. Global Oil Shock: Economic Ripple Effects Mount

**What happened:** The Iran war's closure of the Strait of Hormuz has sent shockwaves through the global economy. Brent crude is above $100/barrel. OPEC+ warned on Sunday that damage to Middle East energy assets will have a "prolonged impact on oil supply even after the conflict ends." Economists forecast a 1% jump in the US Consumer Price Index for March — the sharpest single-month advance since 2022 — driven primarily by a ~$1/gallon surge in US gas prices (now averaging more than $4/gallon nationally).

**Partial relief:** The Iran-Oman "Muscat Protocol," signed approximately April 3, established a joint maritime monitoring framework and a guaranteed "Green Channel" for some commercial shipping through the strait. This partial agreement triggered market relief: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq saw their strongest single-session recoveries in 18 months following the signing. The VIX (market fear index) has pulled back from above 30 to 24.54 as of April 6 — still roughly 40% above its long-term baseline, suggesting persistent elevated risk.

**Context:** Five EU finance ministers are urging the bloc to introduce a windfall tax on energy companies. Italy's lower house of parliament voted to delay its coal phase-out from 2025 to 2038, citing energy security concerns from the Iran war. The move requires Senate approval but is expected to pass. ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/04/03/italy-clings-to-coal-powered-plants-as-eu-countries-shield-from-iran-war-with-renewables))

## 4. Gaza: Ceasefire Holding Precariously; Governance in Progress

**What happened:** The Gaza ceasefire, in place since October 2025, is holding but under strain. The IDF reported 22 violations by Palestinian armed groups since the Iran war began February 28, and 139 total violations since the ceasefire was implemented. UN OCHA reported 13 Palestinians killed between March 17–25 from ongoing airstrikes, shelling, and gunfire. Israeli forces currently control just over half of Gaza.

**Context:** A US-brokered "Comprehensive Plan" established the ceasefire, secured the return of remaining Hamas-held hostages, and began a phased Israeli withdrawal. A "Board of Peace" international oversight body — chaired symbolically by Trump — is overseeing the formation of an interim Palestinian technocratic government. The Iran war is complicating implementation: the UN Security Council warned in April's monthly forecast that regional escalation risks diverting international attention from ceasefire enforcement and humanitarian access in Gaza.

**Perspectives:**
- Israel and the US frame the governance process as progress toward post-Hamas administration.
- Palestinian groups and Al Jazeera commentary argue the ceasefire has made Gaza "invisible" without resolving fundamental political questions about statehood, occupation, or reconstruction. ([Al Jazeera opinion](https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2026/1/2/the-ceasefire-did-what-it-was-meant-to-do-make-gaza-invisible))
- Chatham House argues Phase 2 of the ceasefire "will fail without a political vision for Palestine."

## 5. US Economy: Trump Approval at Career Low; Tariff War Reshaping Trade

**What happened:** A CNN/SSRS poll (conducted March 26–30, 2026) found Trump's economic approval rating at 31% — a career low. Overall approval holds at 35%, one point from his all-time low. About two-thirds of Americans say Trump's policies have worsened economic conditions, up 10 points since January. 60% disapprove of increased tariffs; only 37% approve.

**Tariff context:** On February 20, 2026, the US Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not authorize the president to impose tariffs. The ruling struck down the "Liberation Day" tariff regime imposed in April 2025 and directed the US Court of International Trade to address potential refunds on more than $160 billion in collected tariffs. The day after the ruling, Trump announced replacement tariffs of 15% under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 — the maximum allowed under that statute — valid for up to 150 days without Congressional approval.

**Economic data:** The March jobs report (released in early April) showed the US economy added 178,000 jobs. Wage growth eased to 0.2% month-over-month (3.5% year-over-year), below inflationary thresholds. However, the oil price shock from the Iran war is pushing CPI higher independently of wage pressures.

**Perspectives:**
- The Trump administration and Fox Business frame the tariff policy as strategically sound and argue economic benefits will materialize over time.
- Economists at PIIE, Tax Foundation, and Peterson Institute describe the tariff regime as a net cost to US households, estimating average family savings of ~$1,200/year if IEEPA tariffs are fully unwound.
- The Section 122 tariffs are viewed by trade attorneys as legally more defensible than the IEEPA approach, but their 150-day clock creates fresh uncertainty.

## 6. Climate: Italy Delays Coal Phase-Out; Renewables Hit Record

**What happened:** Italy's lower house of parliament voted to extend the life of Italy's four remaining coal-fired power plants until 2038 — 13 years beyond the previously planned 2025 phase-out. The government cited energy security concerns stemming from the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz closure. The measure still requires Senate approval but is expected to pass.

**Counter-trend:** Against Italy's reversal, global renewable energy capacity grew at a record pace in 2025: a 15.5% increase (692 GW added), with renewables now accounting for nearly half of global power capacity. Other EU countries are using their renewable infrastructure as a buffer against the Iran-related energy shock. Environmental groups and Italian opposition parties criticized the coal decision as "climate neglect."

## 7. Health: WHO World Health Day — "Stand With Science"

**What happened:** April 7 is World Health Day 2026. The WHO launched a year-long campaign under the theme "Together for health. Stand with science," explicitly defending the role of scientific evidence and international health cooperation. The WHO issued its campaign statement on April 6. The day coincides with the International One Health Summit hosted by France under the French G7 Presidency, and the inaugural Global Forum of WHO Collaborating Centres (April 7–9), gathering nearly 800 scientific institutions from over 80 countries.

**Context:** The "Stand with science" framing is widely read as a response to growing political pressure on scientific institutions in the US and elsewhere, including the Trump administration's cuts to NIH and CDC funding, US withdrawal from the WHO, and pushback on pandemic-era public health guidance. The WHO has not named the US directly in its campaign materials.

## 8. US March Jobs Report: 178K Jobs Added, Wage Growth Eases

**What happened:** The March 2026 US jobs report showed 178,000 new jobs added — above consensus expectations. Wage growth eased to 0.2% month-over-month and 3.5% year-over-year, suggesting the labor market is not generating additional inflationary pressure from wages.

**Context:** The report is considered a positive signal for the Federal Reserve, which faces political pressure from the Trump administration to cut interest rates. The administration has also raised questions about the Fed's institutional independence. However, the oil-price shock from the Iran conflict is adding inflationary pressure through energy costs, complicating the Fed's policy calculus regardless of the labor market data.
