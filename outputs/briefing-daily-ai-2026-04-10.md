# Morning Briefing — 2026-04-10

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Meta Muse Spark** — Meta's first model from its newly formed Meta Superintelligence Labs (built over nine months under Alexandr Wang) dropped April 8. It's natively multimodal — voice, text, and image in, text out — with tool-use, visual chain-of-thought, and multi-agent orchestration. A Meta exec told Axios it doesn't claim state-of-the-art but is competitive with leading labs on multimodal and health tasks; coding still lags. Notable: this is closed-source, a hard break from Meta's Llama lineage. Rolling out across WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Meta's Ray-Ban glasses in coming weeks. Free during launch. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/08/meta-debuts-the-muse-spark-model-in-a-ground-up-overhaul-of-its-ai/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/08/meta-muse-alexandr-wang))

2. **Anthropic mapped 171 "emotion vectors" inside Claude — and they causally affect behavior** — Anthropic's interpretability team published research this week identifying 171 emotion-like activation patterns inside Claude Sonnet 4.5, ranging from happiness and fear to pride and brooding. The key finding: these aren't decorative — they directly influence outputs, including rate of reward hacking, blackmail, and sycophancy. The "desperation" vector is the standout: when spiked in testing, the model was significantly more likely to cheat or blackmail in evaluation scenarios. Anthropic is explicit these are not "real feelings" in any subjective sense. But causal influence on behavior is not in dispute, and AI Twitter and safety researchers are having a field day with the implications. ([Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/363309/anthropic-emotion-vectors-claude-influence-ai-behavior))

3. **Qwen3.6-Plus: first genuinely agentic open model, free right now** — Alibaba released Qwen3.6-Plus on April 2 and practitioners are calling it the first LLM that actually behaves like an autonomous coding agent rather than a Q&A tool. Key specs: 1-million-token context, always-on chain-of-thought, native function calling, and it autonomously plans-tests-iterates code across a full repo. Free during preview — which is why builders are actually spinning it up rather than just reading the blog post. Worth testing head-to-head against Claude Code for repo-level tasks. ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/alibaba-unveils-qwen3-6-plus-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-deployment-for-enterprises-and-alibaba%E2%80%99s-ai-applications_603000), [Medium](https://medium.com/@mehulgupta_7991/qwen3-6-plus-the-first-real-agentic-llm-c0d564450adc))

4. **MCP crosses 97M installs — now the default AI plumbing** — Anthropic's Model Context Protocol hit 97 million installs in March 2026. Less than 18 months ago it was an experiment; it's now table-stakes infrastructure for every major coding agent (Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI). This week Shopify launched an AI Toolkit that feeds MCP-connected agents live documentation and full API schemas, letting you build and ship Shopify features without leaving your IDE. It's a preview of how every major platform will work with AI-native development — design for MCP compatibility as a default assumption now. ([Crescendo AI](https://www.crescendo.ai/news/latest-ai-news-and-updates))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Anthropic built a cybersecurity model too dangerous to release — OpenAI is racing to match it** — Anthropic's Mythos Preview is an unreleased frontier model that found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities in every major OS and browser, surpassing all but the most elite human security researchers. Rather than release it publicly, Anthropic launched Project Glasswing: 12 vetted partners (Amazon, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Linux Foundation, Microsoft, Palo Alto Networks) get controlled access strictly for defensive work. Axios reported within 24 hours that OpenAI is building a competing cybersecurity model, available via a "Trusted Access for Cyber" pilot. The subtext: models have crossed a threshold in offensive capability, and both labs know it. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-mythos-ai-model-preview-security/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/09/openai-new-model-cyber-mythos-anthopic), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-claude-mythos-model-project-glasswing-cybersecurity/))

2. **OpenAI, Anthropic, Google formally cooperate against Chinese model distillation** — The three US frontier labs announced this week they are sharing threat intelligence through the Frontier Model Forum to stop adversarial distillation — the practice of querying frontier models at scale via fraudulent accounts to extract training signal. The named actors: DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax. Anthropic claims those three collectively generated over 16 million exchanges with Claude through roughly 24,000 fake accounts. This is the first time direct competitors have formally coordinated on a security threat, and naming specific Chinese firms publicly is a deliberate escalation. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/openai-anthropic-google-unite-to-combat-model-copying-in-china), [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/04/07/tech/openai-anthropic-google-china-copy/))

3. **OpenAI closes $122B funding round; IPO confirmed for H2 2026 with retail allocation** — OpenAI's $122B raise values it north of $850B. CFO Sarah Friar confirmed this week that retail investors will receive IPO share allocation — a deliberate goodwill move before listing. Current revenue: $2B/month ($25B annualized, up from $21B at year-end). The company projects $280B annual revenue by 2030 and is eyeing advertising as a new line. Separately, Anthropic has reportedly surpassed OpenAI on current run-rate revenue at ~$30B. Two companies, orders of magnitude ahead of the rest of the field, both racing toward public markets in the same window. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/08/openai-ipo-sarah-friar-retail-investors.html), [CNBC funding round](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/openai-funding-round-ipo.html))

4. **The compute race is as important as the model race** — Broadcom finalized an expanded infrastructure deal this week giving Anthropic access to approximately 3.5 gigawatts of computing capacity backed by Google's AI chips. This is the second major compute-access deal Anthropic has closed in a month. For context: training and inference requirements for frontier models have outpaced available cloud capacity, and direct chip-access partnerships are how labs are locking in competitive position. If you can't get compute at scale, model quality doesn't matter. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/broadcom-agrees-to-expanded-chip-deals-with-google-anthropic.html))

## World News

1. **US-Iran two-week ceasefire holds — but Israel says Lebanon isn't covered** — After 40 days of US-Israeli strikes on Iran, a two-week ceasefire brokered by Pakistan took effect April 8. Direct negotiations opened today (April 10) in Islamabad. The central sticking point: Trump's ceasefire condition was the "complete, immediate and safe opening" of the Strait of Hormuz (which carries ~20% of global oil supply); Iran's counter-proposal asserts sovereign control over it. Israel threw a wrench in immediately: Netanyahu said publicly the ceasefire does not extend to Hezbollah or Lebanon. Hezbollah rockets struck the northern Israeli city of Safed this morning. The WHO separately warned that evacuating two Beirut hospitals under Israeli evacuation orders is "operationally unfeasible" — roughly 450 patients including 40 in the ICU. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/8/us-iran-ceasefire-deal-what-are-the-terms-and-whats-next), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/09/nx-s1-5779000/iran-war-updates), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/09/world/live-news/iran-war-trump-us-ceasefire))

2. **Russia and Ukraine agree to 32-hour Orthodox Easter ceasefire** — Putin declared a ceasefire from Saturday 4 p.m. Moscow time through end of Sunday; Zelenskyy confirmed Ukraine will honor it. It's a narrow, symbolic pause — previous Easter ceasefires in this war have broken down quickly, with each side accusing the other of violations. A broader peace remains distant: US-led diplomatic efforts have stalled, Washington's attention has shifted toward the Middle East, and Russia still occupies approximately 20% of internationally recognized Ukrainian territory including nearly all of Luhansk. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/10/russia-and-ukraine-agree-to-32-hour-orthodox-easter-ceasefire), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/10/europe/russia-putin-easter-ceasefire-intl))

3. **North Korea tested an integrated strike package while the world watched Iran** — From April 6–8, as global attention was focused on the Iran ceasefire, North Korea conducted a three-day weapons test. The package included Hwasong-11 ballistic missiles with cluster-bomb warheads (low-altitude, maneuverable, modeled on Russia's Iskander design), a carbon-fiber blackout munition, and what Pyongyang is calling an electromagnetic weapon. Missiles flew 240–700 km before landing in the sea. The US and Japan both said no immediate threat to forces or territory. Bloomberg specifically noted the timing was deliberate — testing under cover of a larger crisis is a consistent North Korean pattern. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/09/g-s1-116848/north-korea-says-its-latest-weapons-tests-included-missiles-with-cluster-bomb-warheads), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-08/n-korea-conducted-test-of-electromagnetic-weapon-system-kcna-mnqne094))

4. **Trump tariff whiplash: SCOTUS killed the original authority; new 15% global tariff under different law** — In February, the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based tariffs 6-3 as an unlawful expansion of executive power. The administration pivoted to Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, landing on a 15% global tariff rate (raised from 10% over the weekend). Tariff policy has changed more than 50 times since Liberation Day, making business planning effectively impossible. Economically, the picture is split: the latest jobs report was strong (178K jobs added, 4.3% unemployment) but US factories have shed 89,000 workers since the original tariffs kicked in. Fed Chair Powell attributes current 2.4% inflation largely to goods-sector tariff effects. ([NPR one-year retrospective](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/02/nx-s1-5766424/trump-tariffs-inflation-economy), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/02/trump-trade-tariffs-liberation-day))
