# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-22

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **Gemini 3.5 Flash — Generally Available Today** — Google's newest model hits GA on May 22, delivering frontier-level intelligence at 4× the output-token speed of comparable models, priced at $1.50/$9 per million tokens. It scores 76.2% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, beating Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding and agentic benchmarks at Flash economics. Developers who got early API access are calling it the best price-to-capability ratio in the current market — the model to reach for if you're building agents that need to call out frequently. ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-io-2026-news/))

2. **OpenClaw's Security Nightmare Goes Public** — The 370k-star GitHub agent hit its inflection point this week as Cisco published findings that made AI Twitter stop cold: CVE-2026-25253 (a one-click RCE where any website can silently hijack your running agent because the local server didn't validate WebSocket origin headers), 135k+ exposed instances on the public internet, and a supply chain attack called ClawHavoc that seeded 800 malicious skills into ClawHub — roughly 20% of the entire registry. Cisco shipped DefenseClaw as a partial mitigation, but the consensus is blunt: patch to 2026.1.29 immediately or stop running it. The broader implication is bigger than OpenClaw — it's the first large-scale demonstration that the "local AI agent with full system access" model has attack surface nobody was taking seriously. ([Cisco Blogs](https://blogs.cisco.com/ai/personal-ai-agents-like-openclaw-are-a-security-nightmare)) ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vulnerability-allows-hackers-to-hijack-openclaw-ai-assistant/))

3. **Gemini Spark — The "AI Running While You Sleep" Model Ships** — Google's Spark is a persistent AI agent running on dedicated VMs, working across Google Workspace, connecting to third-party apps, and eventually managing local files — available now for AI Ultra subscribers at $100/month. It's the clearest production signal yet that background, always-on agents have moved from demo to product. AI Twitter is framing it as what Rabbit R1 and Humane Pin promised and never delivered, now arriving via an app on hardware you already own. ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/))

4. **OpenHuman — The Agent That Reads You First** — An open-source agent project called OpenHuman topped GitHub trending this week by inverting the standard agent playbook: instead of having the agent immediately act, it builds a model of the user's communication style, preferences, and context before doing anything. The approach is getting traction in the second-brain and personal productivity communities as a response to agents that feel generic. Worth watching for anyone building personalized AI products — the "model the user first" framing is a distinct design philosophy from "give the agent better tools." ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/316731/20260516/agent-that-reads-you-first-openhuman-tops-github-trending-inverting-playbook.htm))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **OpenAI Files Confidential S-1 — $1 Trillion Valuation Target** — OpenAI is preparing to file its IPO prospectus with the SEC confidentially as early as today (May 22), with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley advising. The target: a September 2026 listing at $852B–$1T valuation, days after a federal jury in Oakland unanimously rejected all of Elon Musk's claims against the company, removing a major legal overhang. Anthropic is racing to list in October at $900B. Two of the largest IPOs in history could land within weeks of each other — and the window they're both racing toward is the same bull market. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/20/openai-ipo-spacex-musk)) ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/openai-may-file-for-ipo-as-soon-as-friday-fa76e93b))

2. **SpaceX S-1 Exposes Anthropic's Real Burn** — SpaceX's own IPO filing (public this week) revealed that Anthropic is paying SpaceX $1.25 billion per month for GPU compute through May 2029 — a $45B total commitment. The same filing discloses a $60B option to acquire Cursor. These numbers reframe the "Anthropic is heading toward profitability" story: at that burn rate, the $10.9B Q2 2026 revenue projection (130% growth from Q1's $4.8B, reported today) looks less comfortable than the headline suggests. The GPU compute dependency is the hidden variable in every frontier AI company's unit economics. ([Buildfastwithai](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-may-22-2026))

3. **Anthropic Projects First Quarterly Operating Profit** — Separate from the SpaceX disclosure, Anthropic announced today it is on track for its first-ever quarterly operating profit, projecting $10.9B in Q2 2026 revenue. The 130% quarter-over-quarter growth rate is faster than OpenAI's comparable trajectory at the same revenue stage. Combined with the Karpathy hire announced Monday and Claude Code's 46% developer adoption rate (vs. 19% Cursor), this week is the strongest signal yet that the power balance in the frontier race has genuinely shifted from OpenAI's runaway lead to a real three-way competition. ([Buildfastwithai](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-may-22-2026))

4. **The World Is Realizing: Open-Source Agent Security Is Broken** — The OpenClaw crisis isn't just a product story — it's reshaping how the industry thinks about the local agent model. Cisco's DefenseClaw, NVIDIA's OpenShell sandboxing, and OpenAI's Daybreak (launched May 12) all launched within weeks of each other. The emerging picture: every major infra player is rushing to patch a class of vulnerability that nobody took seriously when "run an AI agent on your laptop" was still a hobbyist project. The moment agents got 370k GitHub stars and actual system permissions, they became attack surface. Expect this to be the dominant AI security theme through Q3. ([Cisco Blogs](https://blogs.cisco.com/ai/cisco-announces-defenseclaw)) ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/openai-launches-daybreak-for-ai-powered.html))

## World News

1. **Iran — Strikes Paused, Diplomatic Clock Ticking** — Gulf states (Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE) successfully convinced Trump to call off a scheduled strike on Iran, citing "positive momentum" in Pakistan-led mediation talks. Trump confirmed the decision publicly on May 19, naming the Gulf leaders as the ones who intervened. As of May 22, no deal is signed and the original "two or three day" pause is expiring — negotiations are still live but both sides are signaling limited patience. Iran has not publicly accepted any framework, and the Strait of Hormuz remains a pressure point for global oil markets and the broader G7 economic outlook. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/19/g-s1-122762/trump-says-hes-called-off-iran-strike)) ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-19/trump-says-he-held-off-bombing-iran-after-gulf-states-appealed))

2. **Russia Launches Massive Drone and Missile Barrage at Ukraine** — Russia fired more than 500 drones and dozens of ballistic and cruise missiles at Ukraine in the past 48 hours, one of the largest single barrages of the war. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted a significant share, but multiple cities reported infrastructure damage and casualties. Kyiv described the attack as deliberate escalation following Ukraine's own drone strikes on Russian oil refineries; Russia framed it as retaliation for Ukrainian cross-border attacks. Ceasefire talks that stalled after the expired May 9–11 window remain inactive.

3. **Palestinian Delegation Drops UN Bid After US Visa Threat** — The Palestinian delegation withdrew its campaign for a senior UN post after the U.S. threatened to revoke their diplomatic visas if they continued the push, according to a leaked State Department memo. The move underscores the degree to which US leverage over Palestinian access to international institutions remains a live tool, separate from the active Gaza conflict. The Israeli government also faced domestic backlash this week after its national security minister released taunting videos of flotilla activists detained while attempting to breach Gaza's naval blockade.

4. **Tariff Refunds Flow — Walmart May Pass Savings to Shoppers** — Following a federal court ruling striking down blanket 10% tariffs, roughly $166B in refunds is being distributed to US importers. Walmart executives said this week they are considering directing their refund toward lower store prices, citing consumer stress from high gas costs. G7 finance ministers gathered in Paris to coordinate on the economic aftershocks — disruptions from the tariff reversal, the Iran standoff's effect on oil, and the UN's warning that 2026 is on track for the weakest global growth since the 2008 financial crisis are all on the agenda.
