# Wayloft Competitor Brief — 2026-05-25

## Top 5

1. **CardPointers: on-device Apple Intelligence closes the "double paywall" gap, 50% Spring Sale drops them to $45/yr** — **Threat escalation.** The May 18 brief flagged CardPointers' MCP integration but identified a structural weakness: two paywalls (CardPointers+ + ChatGPT Plus), two accounts, no integrated UX. CardPointers just patched that. Their v7 Apple Intelligence integration runs fully on-device — your card data never leaves your iPhone, no external AI subscription required. Ask Siri "what should I use at Whole Foods" and CardPointers answers from your actual portfolio. On top of that, a 50% Big Spring Sale puts their annual plan at $45/yr — $34/yr cheaper than Wayloft's planned $79/yr Pro tier. The UX moat and price moat Wayloft was counting on are both narrowing in the same week. **What to do:** Ellis Church needs to be visibly better, not just conceptually different — opinionated, portfolio-level, and native. "Better card at checkout" is now commoditized. ([Source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/27/cardpointers-app-can-now-integrate-with-chatgpt-and-more-save-50/), [v7 Review](https://cloud9club.net/card-pointers-review/))

2. **Kudos Premium expands into portfolio-level AI insights — creeping into Wayloft's lane** — **New threat.** Kudos started as a checkout extension ("use this card here"). Their 2026 Premium tier is now materially different: AI bill negotiation (negotiates your phone/cable bills autonomously), automatic activation of all Amex/Chase/Citi/BofA offers across all portals, AI-powered spending insights that surface the wrong-card-at-gas-station and expiring-credits problems, plus subscription deduplication. This is no longer a checkout tool — it's an AI that manages your financial relationship with your cards. Kudos is free-core, $10M funded, and already integrated with 15,000+ retailers. Wayloft's portfolio management and annual fee decision features now have a well-funded incumbent creeping upmarket from below. **What to do:** Wayloft needs to be positioned as the *card decision layer* (should I hold this card at all?) vs. Kudos' *spend optimization layer* (given I have this card, where do I use it?). These are adjacent, not identical — but the messaging separation needs to be crisp before the distinction blurs. ([Source](https://www.joinkudos.com/blog/introducing-kudos-premium-put-your-wallet-on-autopilot))

3. **NerdWallet, TPG, and Bankrate own 62%+ of AI answer citations for credit cards — organic traffic is an obsolete success metric** — **Structural threat to Wayloft's SEO strategy.** Wayloft's entire Layer 1 distribution plan is built on SEO: worth-it SSG pages targeting "[card name] worth the annual fee" queries. New data this week: those three publishers are cited in more than 62% of AI-generated answers for credit card queries. NerdWallet's own CEO confirmed this shift — they lost 20% of web traffic in 2025 even while growing revenue 35%, because Google AI Overviews are answering questions without click-through. If NerdWallet — with decades of domain authority and hundreds of editors — is losing 20% to AI answers, Wayloft's newly-minted SSG pages face a steeper climb. The weekly AI briefing (May 25) framed it directly: "AI didn't upgrade search, it replaced it." Wayloft's SEO window is finite and shortening. **What to do:** Publish the worth-it pages immediately — every week without them live is forgone ranking AND forgone AI training data. Then measure AI citation share, not just Google position. Ask ChatGPT and Claude "is the Amex Gold worth the annual fee" and audit where Wayloft appears. If it's not in the answer, the SEO strategy needs a citation-building layer added. ([Source](https://debanked.com/2025/08/nerdwallet-ai-answers-are-taking-organic-search-clicks-away/), [AI Visibility Index](https://www.5wpr.com/ai-visibility-index/credit-cards-ai-visibility-index-2026/))

4. **Seats.aero ships Seat Map Viewer (Pro beta) and hits 24 programs — anchors $9.99/mo as the travel rewards "pro tool" price** — **Neutral with pricing signal.** Seats.aero added a Seat Map Viewer in Pro beta: enter any flight, pull real GDS seat data, see which seats are open across all cabins. Combined with Fare Class Viewer (which booking codes are available), JetBlue Mint upgrade finder, and 24 airline programs, Seats.aero Pro ($9.99/mo) is the clearest benchmark for what "premium travel rewards tool" means at $10/mo. Wayloft's Pro tier at $9.99/mo or $79/yr is correctly price-anchored against this. No lane overlap — Seats.aero is award search; Wayloft is card portfolio — but the feature-per-dollar comparison is relevant for conversion. **What to do:** Nothing urgent. But if Wayloft's Pro landing page is ever live, reference Seats.aero's feature set as the implicit "that's what $10/mo gets you over there; here's what it gets you here." ([Source](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/seats-aero-adds-new-features/), [Seats.aero Pro](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/seats-aero-free-vs-pro/))

5. **Alaska Airlines' Atmos Rewards wins TPG Innovation Award; Bank of America expands partnership** — **Watch.** Alaska overhauled its loyalty program (now Atmos Rewards) and won The Points Guy's 2026 innovation award. Key new feature: the Global Companion Award, a premium redemption type. Alaska and Bank of America also announced expanded co-brand credit card partnership. Alaska Mileage Plan is already a Wayloft transfer partner data point (Bank of America co-brand). If Atmos Rewards sees the mainstream attention of a Bilt-style growth arc, Wayloft's data needs to reflect the new program name, earn structure, and redemption types accurately before users start searching. Minor update now, potential data gap later. ([Source](https://news.alaskaair.com/loyalty/atmos-rewards-earns-the-points-guys-top-honors-for-innovation-and-credit-card-excellence/), [BofA partnership](https://news.alaskaair.com/company/alaska-air-group-and-bank-of-america-expand-partnership/))

## User Pain Points

- **Annual fee fatigue remains the loudest unserved signal.** "Is Amex Platinum still worth it after the latest refresh?" is the recurring Reddit post. No competitor has an indexed, shareable, public answer — they all require login or manual calculation. Wayloft's keep/call/downgrade verdict is the answer this user is searching for. The pages just need to exist and be findable.
- **CardPointers' price drop creates a benchmark problem.** At $45/yr (sale), users will comparison-shop. Wayloft's free tier needs to be genuinely useful — not a teaser — to earn the upgrade ask at $79/yr. The gap between what's free and what's $79 needs to be crystal clear and defensible.
- **"Which card earns the most on groceries/dining/travel" is still a manual exercise.** MaxRewards and CardPointers both answer this at checkout, but neither answers it pre-portfolio — i.e., "if I got the Amex Gold, would it beat what I already have?" Wayloft's card recommendation engine answers this; it just needs to be discoverable.
- **Loyalty devaluation is untracked.** No tool alerts users when a program devalues (e.g., United raises saver award prices, Hyatt adds dynamic pricing to more properties). Users find out when they go to book. This is a "lock-in" feature — once a user gets a devaluation alert that saves them 50,000 points, they never cancel. Still completely uncontested. (Flagged in Weekly Opportunities scan May 25.)

## Watch List

- **MaxRewards AI integration is still pending.** The May 18 brief predicted 60-90 days. Now at day 7 of that window. CardPointers' on-device AI will pressure MaxRewards to respond. Watch for a May or June announcement.
- **Google AI Mode eroding SEO as a distribution channel.** Not just NerdWallet's problem — every SEO-first content strategy needs a citation-building layer added alongside it. Wayloft should be tracking whether its pages are in AI answers, not just search results.
- **Point.me adding portfolio tracker.** Noted in the May 25 opportunities scan. Point.me ($12/mo) historically focused on award search; a portfolio tracker would bring them directly into Wayloft's territory. Confirm scope before assuming threat.
- **CardPointers Android v7 beta.** Currently iOS-only for the full feature set. Android release will significantly expand their addressable market. Watch timing.
- **PointsYeah hotel + flight consolidation.** Still watch — if they add a "which card earns the most for this trip" layer, the scope creep into Wayloft territory becomes real.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Ship the worth-it SSG pages this week, not next.** The AI citation window is finite. Every day without indexed pages is a day Wayloft is invisible to both Google and AI answers. The pages exist in the codebase — they need to be live. This is the single most important distribution action available right now.

2. **Audit AI answers for Wayloft's target queries.** Ask ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini: "Is the Amex Gold worth the annual fee?" "Is the Chase Sapphire Preferred worth $95/year?" Log what they say, what sources they cite. This is Wayloft's new SEO audit — measuring AI citation share. If Wayloft doesn't appear, the content or domain authority isn't there yet.

3. **Reconsider Pro pricing in light of CardPointers $45/yr sale.** $79/yr is 75% more expensive. That's defensible only if the value delta is obvious. The free tier needs to be genuinely useful (worth-it tool, card recs, basic portfolio) and the Pro upgrade needs to feel like a step-change, not a gate. Consider whether $59/yr or a 30% launch discount makes the positioning stronger at launch.

4. **Sharpen the Ellis Church vs. Kudos positioning.** Kudos optimizes how you spend with the cards you have. Ellis Church helps you decide which cards to hold in the first place. This is the upstream/downstream distinction — but it needs to be stated on the landing page, not just understood internally. "Kudos tells you which card to swipe. Ellis Church tells you which cards to keep."

5. **Add Atmos Rewards to the transfer partner database.** Low-effort, data completeness. Alaska Mileage Plan becoming Atmos Rewards is a rename with new earn/redemption structures. Update Wayloft's data before users notice the gap.
