# Morning Briefing — 2026-05-29

## AI Tools, Tech & Advancements

1. **OpenAI Daybreak** — OpenAI quietly launched a dedicated cybersecurity platform on May 11 built on three tiers of GPT-5.5: standard, Trusted Access for Cyber (defensive use in authorized environments), and GPT-5.5-Cyber (full red-team/pen-test permissive mode). It uses Codex Security to map realistic attack paths in a repo, find and test vulns in isolation, then propose patches. CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Cloudflare, and Cisco are already integrating it. Access is still invite-only, but this is OpenAI's clearest move into the enterprise security stack. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/openai-launches-daybreak-for-ai-powered.html))

2. **Claude Code at 46% — the survey is in** — The Pragmatic Engineer's 2026 developer survey is out and the headline is brutal for competitors: Claude Code sits at 46% as the most-loved AI coding tool, with Cursor at 19% and GitHub Copilot at 9%. 72% of developers now use AI coding tools daily. 41% of all code written globally is AI-generated. Claude Code went from zero to category leader in eight months, and Anthropic's internal figure of $2.5B annualized revenue from Claude Code alone backs the numbers up. The model war is over for now — the workflow war is what matters. ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026))

3. **Alteryx Agent Studio + MCP** — At Inspire 2026, Alteryx launched Agent Studio, which lets business analysts convert existing Alteryx workflows and business logic directly into deployable autonomous agents via an MCP Server. This is the first major enterprise data tool to turn legacy workflow investments into agents without needing engineers to rewrite them. If it works as advertised, it's a meaningful path for non-technical teams to get into agents. ([Composio](https://composio.dev/content/top-ai-workflow-automation-tools-you-must-not-miss-in-2026))

4. **The workflow > model realization is consolidating** — The developer conversation this week has measurably shifted from "which model?" to "which setup?" Power users on r/ClaudeAI and dev.to are sharing that Sonnet 4.6 with structured planning outperforms Opus 4.7 used naively. n8n (self-hostable), Composio (developer integration layer), and Claude Code are being combined into custom orchestration stacks. The value is in the workflow, not the frontier model. This is the mature phase of the vibe coding era. ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/marxon/the-best-developer-ai-tools-of-2026-q1-what-actually-changed-in-real-workflows-25l3))

## AI Industry News & Shifts

1. **Anthropic is closing a $900B valuation round this week** — Anthropic is in final talks to raise $30 billion at a valuation exceeding $900 billion — nearly tripling its $380 billion valuation from February's Series G, which was only three months ago. The round is expected to close by end of May. The business case: annualized revenue has hit $14 billion, Claude Code alone is generating $2.5B, and Anthropic projects $10.9B in Q2 revenue and a $50B annualized run rate by end of June. For context, this would give Anthropic a higher valuation than Walmart. The speed of this rerating is historically unusual for any private company. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-12/anthropic-in-talks-to-raise-30-billion-at-900-billion-valuation), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/openai-co-founder-andrej-karpathy-joins-anthropics-pre-training-team/))

2. **Anthropic is now beating OpenAI on enterprise adoption** — A new report from Morningstar shows Anthropic at 34.4% enterprise adoption vs. OpenAI's 32.3% — a flip from a year ago. The driver is overwhelmingly Claude Code and coding workflows; Anthropic is winning developer-adjacent enterprise seats first, then expanding. OpenAI still leads on consumer ChatGPT and brand recognition, but the revenue race is now close: Anthropic at ~$14B annualized, OpenAI at ~$25B as of February. The gap is closing fast. ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260528ny46821/openai-and-anthropic-dominate-ai-revenue-and-communicate-very-differently))

3. **AI job displacement is becoming concrete, not theoretical** — Dario Amodei went on record this month projecting that AI could eliminate 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman put a timeline on it: 18 months to automate most professional work. This followed Block (Jack Dorsey's company) cutting nearly 5,000 people — half its workforce — in February and explicitly citing AI as the reason. Anthropic's own economic research mapped which roles are most exposed: junior financial analysts, entry-level legal research, and routine knowledge work are the first wave. The debate has shifted from "if" to "how fast" and "what to do about it." ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/06/ai-job-losses-report-anthropic-research-great-recession-for-white-collar-workers/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/26/nx-s1-5727687/is-ai-really-coming-for-white-collar-jobs))

## World News

1. **US-Iran: tentative deal reached, Trump hasn't signed yet** — A tentative agreement between the US and Iran was reached yesterday (May 28), but Trump has not formally signed off. The proposed deal: a 60-day ceasefire extension, Iran clears mines from the Strait of Hormuz and allows free passage, the US lifts its port blockade and issues sanctions waivers allowing Iran to sell oil. In exchange, Iran would commit to not pursuing nuclear weapons and begin formal negotiations on its enriched uranium stockpile. Trump says the deal is "largely negotiated" but is "not satisfied" with current terms. Iran's IRGC fired warning shots at vessels near the strait yesterday, calling it a show of force — not a ceasefire violation. The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly 20% of global oil supply; any deal or breakdown has immediate commodity market implications. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/28/world/live-news/iran-war-us-news), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/23/us-iran-war-talks.html), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/24/iran-deal-strait-hormuz-sanctions-nuclear))

2. **Gaza ceasefire fraying at the edges** — Hamas accused Israel this week of moving the ceasefire demarcation line — the "yellow line" — further into Gaza territory, claiming it violates the October 2025 agreement. Under that deal, Israeli forces withdrew to a position leaving them in roughly 53% of Gaza. Hamas says the line has shifted and is demanding clarification. Israel has not publicly responded to the specific allegation. The stabilization force that was announced three months ago to hold the ceasefire still has not materialized. No shooting has resumed but the political situation is deteriorating. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/28/world/live-news/iran-war-us-news), [Wikipedia — Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war))

3. **US tariffs: Supreme Court struck down the main authority, Trump pivoted** — The Supreme Court ruled earlier this month that Trump exceeded his authority using the 1977 International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose sweeping reciprocal tariffs. Trump responded by pivoting to Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, imposing a flat 10% tariff on all imports. The Tax Foundation estimates this is the largest US tax increase as a share of GDP since 1993 — averaging $1,500 per household annually. April inflation came in at 3.8% year-on-year (core at 2.8%), and Goldman Sachs projects tariffs will add another 1% to inflation through mid-2026. The unemployment rate is holding at 4.3% for now, but economists expect it to drift up. ([Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/), [SF Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2026/03/effects-of-tariffs-on-components-of-inflation/))

4. **China's Shenzhou 23 astronaut crew includes year-long mission** — China launched the Shenzhou 23 spacecraft with three astronauts bound for its Tiangong space station. One astronaut is staying for a full year — a first for China — to study human adaptability in long-duration spaceflight. This is a direct parallel to NASA/Roscosmos year-long mission research, and positions China for eventual crewed lunar and deep space operations. The launch comes amid ongoing US restrictions on NASA-China collaboration. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/world/))
