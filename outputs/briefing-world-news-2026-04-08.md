## 1. US-Iran War: Two-Week Ceasefire Agreed — Day 40

**What happened:** After 40 days of US-Israeli strikes on Iran, a two-week ceasefire took effect on April 7–8. The deal was brokered by Pakistan. Iran's Supreme National Security Council formally accepted the terms. Both Israel and the US announced suspension of attacks.

**Terms:**
- US and Israel halt all strikes on Iran for two weeks.
- Iran agrees to "complete, immediate, and safe opening" of the Strait of Hormuz, with vessels coordinating with Iranian armed forces during the period.
- Formal negotiations to begin in Islamabad on or around April 11.

**Iran's stated conditions:** Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran's "powerful armed forces will cease their defensive operations" if attacks stop. Iran's 10-point position also includes easing of US sanctions and withdrawal of US combat forces from regional bases — framed as negotiating positions, not preconditions to the ceasefire.

**Iran's framing:** Iran declared the outcome a "victory." The US said its military objectives had been "largely achieved." Both framings are contested.

**Background:** Trump had set an 8 PM ET April 7 deadline threatening "a whole civilization will die tonight" if the Strait remained closed. Pakistan's PM Shehbaz Sharif brokered the last-hour compromise, asking Trump to delay the deadline two weeks and asking Iran to open Hormuz as a "goodwill gesture."

**What's uncertain:** Whether the two-week window converts to a durable agreement. Key unresolved issues include sanctions, US regional force posture, and Iran's nuclear program.

## 2. Markets: Oil Plunges, Stocks Rally on Ceasefire News

**What happened:** Global markets responded sharply to the ceasefire announcement.

**Oil:**
- West Texas Intermediate (WTI) fell as much as 19% — the largest single-day drop in roughly six years.
- Brent crude dropped ~14%, sliding below $100/barrel (to approximately $94).
- Prior to ceasefire: EIA had raised its 2026–2027 oil forecasts and projected US gasoline prices peaking at $4.30/gallon in April.

**Equities:**
- Dow futures +1,000+ points; S&P 500 futures +2.5%; Nasdaq 100 futures +~3%.
- Japan's Nikkei +4.8%; South Korea's Kospi +5.6%.
- On April 7 (before ceasefire confirmed after hours): S&P 500 closed up 0.08% at 6,616.85; Nasdaq +0.10% at 22,017.85; Dow -0.18% at 46,584.46.

**Context:** Markets had been under pressure from both the Iran war (oil shock, flight disruption, supply chain uncertainty) and the broader tariff environment. Ceasefire relief is the dominant driver of the April 8 rally.

## 3. Ukraine-Russia: Peace Talks Stalled, Iran War Displacing Attention

**What happened:** The Kremlin confirmed on April 6 that trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia negotiations are "on pause." Both Russia and Ukraine continue bilateral contacts with Washington, but no three-party meeting is scheduled.

**Russian position:** Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stated on April 7 that Ukraine's latest draft treaty was "unacceptable" and diverged from terms negotiators had previously agreed on. Lavrov said Russia's delegation would continue the process "despite all provocations."

**Ukrainian position:** Zelenskyy acknowledged that the Iran war is absorbing Trump administration attention, putting US-brokered peace talks on indefinite hold. He warned separately that a protracted Middle East war could reduce air defense availability for Ukraine. Zelenskyy held talks with US negotiators Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, Senator Lindsey Graham, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte on April 1.

**Battlefield context:** Putin has demanded Ukraine withdraw from remaining Donbas territory his forces have not yet seized. Zelenskyy has refused to cede territory Ukraine still holds militarily. No significant frontline change reported April 7–8.

**What's uncertain:** Whether peace talks resume after the Iran ceasefire window, and on what terms.

## 4. Gaza: Ceasefire Holding Tenuously, Hamas Rejects Disarmament Demand

**What happened:** The US-brokered Gaza ceasefire (which took effect October 2025) remains nominally in effect but is under strain.

**IDF figures:** Palestinian groups have violated the ceasefire 22 times since the start of the Iran war (February 28–April 6), with 139 total violations since the truce began in October.

**Hamas position:** Hamas's armed wing rejected calls to disarm, stating disarmament is non-negotiable until Israel fully implements Phase 1 of the ceasefire and guarantees complete withdrawal from Gaza. Hamas told mediators it would not discuss disarmament without those guarantees.

**US position:** Trump's 20-point Gaza peace plan is in Phase 2, which requires Hamas to totally disarm — the core sticking point.

**Humanitarian note (context from earlier searches):** Over one in ten of Gaza's pre-war population has been killed or injured since October 2023; the majority are homeless. These figures predate the April 7–8 window but remain the backdrop.

**What's uncertain:** Whether the Iran ceasefire creates diplomatic bandwidth to push Gaza negotiations forward, or whether Phase 2 remains deadlocked indefinitely.

## 5. US Trade Policy: IEEPA Tariffs Struck Down (Feb 20), Section 122 Replacement Active

**What happened (ongoing context):** On February 20, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 (*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*) that IEEPA does not authorize the president to impose tariffs. Chief Justice Roberts wrote that IEEPA "contains no reference to tariffs or duties" and that "until now no President has read IEEPA to confer such power." The majority included Gorsuch and Barrett.

**Aftermath:** Trump announced replacement tariffs under Section 122 of the Trade Act. The effective tariff rate has risen from 2.1% to an estimated 11.7% as of early 2026 — the largest US tax increase as a share of GDP since 1993. Average per-household tax impact estimated at $1,500 in 2026.

**April context:** Trump raged publicly at the Court in late March. The Iran war has partially shifted political attention away from the tariff fight, but the economic impact (inflation, supply chain costs, mortgage rate increases) continues. PCE inflation is forecast at 2.9% for 2026 due in part to energy and tariff effects.

**What's uncertain:** Whether Section 122 tariffs face their own legal challenge; whether the Supreme Court will weigh in on refunds for importers who paid IEEPA tariffs (estimated >$200B collected in 2025).

## 6. Vietnam: New Prime Minister Elected

**What happened:** On April 7, Vietnam's National Assembly unanimously elected Le Minh Hung as Prime Minister for the 2026–2031 term. All 495 deputies present voted in favor.

**Who is Le Minh Hung:** Born 1970, Ha Tinh province. Former Governor of the State Bank of Vietnam (2016–2020). Most recently served as Head of the Party Central Committee's Organization Commission. Holds a master's degree in Public Policy.

**His stated priorities:** Annual GDP growth of at least 10% during 2026–2031 driven by innovation and digital transformation; modern service-oriented government; investment in human development and social welfare.

**Context:** The US State Department formally congratulated Vietnam on the elections of both President To Lam and Prime Minister Le Minh Hung on April 8.

## 7. WHO World Health Day: "Stand with Science" Campaign Launched April 7

**What happened:** The World Health Organization launched World Health Day 2026 on April 7 under the theme "Together for health. Stand with science." Two anchor events were held in Lyon, France: the International One Health Summit (hosted by France's G7 Presidency) and the inaugural Global Forum of WHO Collaborating Centres (800+ institutions from 80+ countries).

**Key stats cited by WHO:**
- Global maternal mortality rate down more than 40% since 2000.
- Deaths in children under five reduced by over 50% since 2000.
- Dengue fever at its largest global outbreak on record — WHO attributed spread to rising temperatures expanding mosquito habitats.

**Political subtext:** The "Stand with science" framing is widely read as a response to US federal vaccine and science policy shifts under the current administration. From June 2025 to March 2026, public trust in federal childhood vaccine recommendations dropped 11 percentage points to 60%; approximately one in six US parents now report delaying or skipping some vaccinations.

## 8. Iran War Regional Spillover: Travel Disruption, Ukraine Ripple Effects

**Two secondary effects worth tracking:**

**Travel disruption:** Russia's Foreign Ministry reported approximately 50,000 Russian tourists are stranded across the Middle East as the Iran war disrupts air travel. Popular destinations including Dubai, Qatar, and Iran-adjacent routes are affected by mass flight delays and cancellations. Broader international flight disruption from Hormuz-area conflict is ongoing but expected to ease with ceasefire.

**Ukraine air defense concern:** Zelenskyy publicly acknowledged that a protracted Middle East war could reduce the availability of Western air defense systems for Ukraine, as US and allied attention and munitions production are diverted. This is a stated concern, not a confirmed reallocation — no specific system transfers to the Middle East have been publicly confirmed.
